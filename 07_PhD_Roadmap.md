### Phase 3 — Add Appendix A (engagement with the secondary literature) — **NEXT**

**Goal:** Write the appendix that the v1.2 outline removed in order to honor the Bible-only constraint. Reintroduce it for the PhD submission, *clearly bounded* so that the Bible-only core argument is preserved.

**Why the relaxation is necessary:**

A PhD in biblical theology is expected to engage the secondary literature on the topic. The current Bible-only constraint is a methodological *strength* of the project (it commits the argument to the text alone), but in a PhD context it is also a *limitation* that must be honestly addressed. The standard move in the field is to:

1. Conduct the argument *from* the text alone (the main body)
2. Engage the secondary literature *as* the reception history of the text (an appendix)

This way the Bible-only argument is preserved as the *positive contribution*, and the secondary literature is engaged as the *conversation* the contribution enters.

**Appendix A outline:**

- **A.A.1** Federal theology and the covenant of works (Reformed orthodox tradition: Witsius, *The Economy of the Covenants*; Cocceius, *Summa Doctrinae de Foedere*; the Westminster Confession of Faith on the covenant of works)
- **A.A.2** The federal headship tradition (A.A. Hodge, *Outlines of Theology*; John Murray, *The Imputation of Adam's Sin*; the *Westminster Confession of Faith* on federal headship)
- **A.A.3** The anti-federal-headship tradition (seminary-millennial / dispensational critique; Charles Ryrie, *Dispensationalism*; the Socinian tradition as a historical [unitarian] parallel)
- **A.A.4** The two-wills christology (the Chalcedonian formulation; dyothelitism; the Reformed and Lutheran debates on the two natures)
- **A.A.5** The active and passive obedience of Christ (John Murray, *Redemption Accomplished and Applied*; the Reformed orthodox distinction; the modern critical engagement)
- **A.A.6** The state-of-the-dead debate (conditional immortality vs. natural immortality; the Seventh Day Adventist and Christadelphian literature; the Socinian literature)
- **A.A.7** Modern biblical scholarship (Wright, *The Resurrection of the Son of God*; Dunn, *Theology of Paul the Apostle*; the Journal for the Study of the New Testament supplements)
- **A.A.8** The unitarian-biblical-theology tradition (the literature that the project itself draws from — historically unitarian engagement with the Adam-Christ parallel)

**Word-count contribution:** ~20,000 words.

**Estimated effort:** 4–6 sessions.

**Critical note on Appendix A:** The Bible-only constraint is *preserved* in the main body. Appendix A is an *explicit relaxation* of the constraint for the purpose of secondary-literature engagement. The introduction should state this explicitly: "The argument of the main body is conducted from the text alone. Appendix A engages the secondary literature *as* the conversation the text's argument has generated, not as evidence for theological claims. The contribution of the main body is its bible-only argument; the contribution of Appendix A is its mapping of that argument onto the conversation."

## 5. The AI-collaboration disclosure

This manuscript is a product of **human-AI collaboration**. The disclosure is made here, in the project's published state, because the integrity of the work requires it.

**What the AI did:**

- Drafted the prose of Chapters 1, 2, 3, and 15 (and the master's INTRO.md) from a structural outline and a set of research questions provided by the human author.
- Verified biblical quotations against esv.org (using a Python script that fetched the ESV HTML, parsed the verse-span data, and extracted the verified text).
- Identified quotation drifts and other errors in the drafted chapters (e.g., the Hosea 6:6 *ḥesed* / *raḥamim* distinction in Chapter 1, the Romans 2:11 paraphrase in Chapter 1, the Numbers 3:7–8 paraphrase in Chapter 2).
- Drafted the band-tag evidence sketches following the project's epistemic standard (INTRO.md §I.5).
- Drafted the verification logs following the project's verification standard (the pattern established in Chapter 15).
- Drafted this roadmap document.

**What the human did:**

- Provided the working thesis, the research questions, the spine, and the methodological commitments (Bible-only, federal-headship, unitarian) that made the work possible.
- Reviewed each drafted chapter and made the load-bearing decisions: which `[H]` claims to elevate, which evidence-sketch items to include, which translation notes to record.
- Made the section-by-section decisions on emphasis, framing, and the cross-links to downstream chapters.
- Provided the source-constraint judgment that drives the entire methodology (the Bible-only constraint; the *unitarian* rather than Trinitarian perspective; the federal-headship framework).
- Held the project's integrity across the drafting sessions — the AI does not remember prior sessions, and the human's continuity of vision is what kept the project coherent.

**What neither of us did alone:**

- The argument. The *interpretive moves* (the choice of evidence-sketch items, the `[I-high]` vs. `[I-mid]` band assignments, the `[H-mid]` vs. `[H-high]` distinctions) are joint decisions — the AI proposed, the human reviewed, and the band assignments are a *convergent* result of both.

**What this is *not*:**

- It is not the case that the AI generated the argument and the human took credit for it. The argument is the human's; the AI is the drafting tool.
- It is not the case that the human *dictated* and the AI *transcribed*. The drafting was an iterative collaboration in which the AI produced more material than the human would have drafted alone, and the human shaped the material to fit the project's commitments.

**How the disclosure should be cited in the dissertation:**

The dissertation should include a statement in the front matter along the lines of:

> *An earlier draft of this manuscript was produced with the assistance of an AI language model (the drafting tool used in 2026). The AI assisted with biblical-text verification, prose drafting, and the development of the `[E]/[I-band]/[H-band]` tagging system. The interpretive framework, the working thesis, the source-constraint decisions, and the load-bearing exegetical judgments are the author's. The collaboration is documented in the project's `07_PhD_Roadmap.md` file.*

**The honesty of the disclosure is itself a methodological contribution.** A PhD committee evaluating this work can ask: "Which parts of this argument were generated by the AI?" And the answer is honest: the prose was drafted with AI assistance, the bibliographic verification was automated, the band-tag evidence sketches were generated collaboratively, and the *interpretive* decisions (the commitments, the choices, the framework) are the human's. The committee can then evaluate the framework on its merits, which is what the dissertation is supposed to support.

**The disclosure is also a statement of methodological realism.** The Bible-only constraint is one methodological commitment; the AI-collaboration disclosure is another. Both are commitments to *honesty about the work's sources*. The paper's argument is what the Bible says; the paper's authorship is what the human and the AI together produced. Both should be acknowledged.

## 6. Risks and mitigations

**Risk 1: AI-generated prose can mask the author's voice.** A PhD committee should be able to identify the author's voice in the prose. The mitigation is the third-pass copy edit (Phase 6): the author rewrites the prose in their own voice, not the AI's. The AI produces the scaffolding; the author produces the final voice.

**Risk 2: The band-tag evidence sketches can become formulaic.** The `[E]/[I-band]/[H-band]` pattern is a strength, but if every claim has the same 4-bullet structure, the dissertation can read as mechanical. The mitigation is the second-pass deepening (Phase 2): each sketch becomes *its own* argument, with the band's confidence as the conclusion, not the starting point.

**Risk 3: Programs that require engagement with the secondary literature may reject the Bible-only constraint even with Appendix A.** The mitigation is to identify a program whose faculty is sympathetic to the Bible-only approach. Several programs (particularly in conservative evangelical seminaries) have PhD tracks that tolerate this; the dissertation just needs to be clear about the approach.

**Risk 4: The unitarian perspective is unusual in academic biblical theology.** Most PhD programs in biblical theology are at Trinitarian institutions. The unitarian perspective is more common in independent or unitarian-affiliated institutions, and in some graduate programs in religious studies (rather than biblical theology per se). The roadmap does not commit to a specific program; the user should identify programs that can accommodate the unitarian perspective before committing to the PhD path.

**Risk 5: The dissertation defense requires the author to defend the argument in real time.** The `[E]/[I-band]/[H-band]` apparatus makes the argument transparent, but the author must *internalize* the argument — the AI cannot do this for the author. The mitigation is the third-pass copy edit (Phase 6) and the author's own study of the biblical texts in parallel with the drafting. The author must read the texts themselves, repeatedly, until the argument is theirs.

## 7. Decision points

**Before PhD submission, the user must decide:**

1. **Master's vs. PhD target.** If the user is unsure, completing Phases 1–3 (the chapter drafts and Appendix A) yields a manuscript that can be either a master's thesis (~100,000 words) or a PhD dissertation with further Polish and program-specific adaptation.

2. **Bible-only constraint strictness.** The user can either (a) maintain the constraint strictly in the main body and relax it only in Appendix A, or (b) relax the constraint throughout the dissertation. Option (a) is the stronger methodological move; option (b) is more conventional.

3. **Program target.** The user should identify candidate programs and consult their specific requirements before committing to the PhD path. The roadmap's word-count targets are general; the program's requirements may differ.

4. **Time commitment.** The user should commit to the timeline they can sustain. The 30–40 session estimate assumes consistent engagement; gaps longer than 2 weeks between sessions will require re-orientation.

5. **The unitarian framing.** PhD programs in biblical theology are predominantly Trinitarian; the user should identify whether the dissertation's unitarian framing is a feature or a barrier at their target programs. Some programs welcome the unitarian perspective as a corrective to the Trinitarian consensus; others reject it as a non-starter.
