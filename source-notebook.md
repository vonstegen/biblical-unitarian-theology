You've raised one of the central questions in Christian theology: **Does Scripture explicitly teach the Trinity, or is it a theological conclusion drawn from Scripture?**

The answer depends on the perspective being taken.

### Your observation

It is true that the Bible **never uses the word "Trinity"** or "triune God." There is no verse that says:

That formulation was developed by the early church over several centuries as Christians sought to explain all the biblical data while rejecting views they believed were inconsistent with Scripture.

### The classical Christian view

Most Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant theologians argue that while the word *Trinity* is absent, the doctrine is inferred from passages that affirm:

* There is one God (e.g., Deuteronomy 6:4).
* The Father is God.
* The Son is called God or possesses divine attributes in passages such as John 1:1, John 20:28, Hebrews 1:8, and Colossians 2:9.
* The Holy Spirit is described with divine attributes and actions (Acts 5:3–4 is often cited).

From these passages, they conclude the doctrine of the Trinity.

### A non-Trinitarian view

Groups such as Biblical Unitarians, Christadelphians, Jehovah's Witnesses, Oneness Pentecostals (though they differ from Unitarians), and others argue differently. They contend that:

* The doctrine of the Trinity goes beyond what Scripture explicitly states.
* God is the Father alone.
* Jesus is the Messiah and Son of God but should be understood differently than classical Trinitarian theology teaches.
* The Holy Spirit is God's Spirit or power rather than a distinct divine person (again, views vary among these groups).

They often emphasize that theology should rest on explicit biblical teaching rather than later creeds.
