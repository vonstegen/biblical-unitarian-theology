# Scripture-First Textual and Historical Research Protocol — v0.1

**Central question:** What do the Hebrew Scriptures, Jesus, and the apostolic authors say about Jesus as prophet, Messiah, Son of God, and King, and how do competing Christological interpretations account for that evidence?

## Evidence procedure
1. Read the passage in its literary and historical context before commentaries.
2. Establish the textual base and named witnesses. Distinguish manuscript variants from translation differences and deliberate scriptural quotations/allusions.
3. Examine Hebrew, Aramaic, or Greek grammar, lexical range, and alternative renderings.
4. Assess the earlier passage's context and its reception in later Scripture and Second Temple Jewish literature.
5. Compare strong biblical Unitarian, classical Trinitarian, and relevant Jewish scholarly readings without presupposing an outcome.
6. State objections and limitations before conclusions.

## Evidence labels
- **[T]** Direct textual observation.
- **[V]** Verified manuscript/edition variant, with witnesses.
- **[L]** Original-language analysis.
- **[H]** Historical inference with sources.
- **[I]** Interpretive/theological inference with alternatives.
- **[Q]** Unresolved question or unverified claim.

## Source roles and safeguards
REV is the primary daily reader, not a neutral arbiter. Finnegan's *Read the Bible for Yourself* informs reading practice, not final doctrinal conclusions. AICNT is a secondary critical translation/apparatus whose witness selection and editorial decisions require independent scrutiny. For the OT, compare Masoretic Hebrew, relevant Dead Sea Scrolls, Septuagint, Samaritan Pentateuch, and other ancient versions as appropriate. Use named critical editions and lexica with clear licensing/provenance.

The repository already contains `aicnt-extension/01_FORMAT_SPEC.md`, `02_OT_EXTENSION_PLAN.md`, and a Tobit pilot. That is a distinct critical-edition effort. This study produces **research dossiers**, not an independent critical edition.

## AI and review controls
AI may propose translations, comparisons, and hypotheses, but cannot claim direct manuscript collation it has not performed. Record exact source/edition, provenance, date checked, reviewer, and verification status. Each dossier requires original-language review, variant log, fair competing interpretations, counterevidence, and owner sign-off before it is marked **accepted**.

Preserve the existing Debt–Debtor thesis documents and their accepted status. This project can test or cite that thesis but does not silently modify it.
