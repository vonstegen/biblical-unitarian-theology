## Preliminary alignment and testable findings

| Theme | Hebrew Deuteronomy (short source phrase) | Greek Deuteronomy (short source phrase) | Greek Acts 3 (short source phrase) | Initial assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prophet raised up (18:15 / Acts 3:22) | `נָבִיא … יָקִים` | `προφήτην … αναστήσει` | `Προφήτην ὑμῖν ἀναστήσει` | Shared promise of a prophet raised up; confirm word order, pronouns and text editions |
| Origin among Israel (18:15 / Acts 3:22) | `מִקִּרְבְּךָ מֵאַחֶיךָ` | `εκ των αδελφών σου` | `ἐκ τῶν ἀδελφῶν ὑμῶν` | Source uses singular address; Acts uses plural; **quotation adaptation**, not automatically a manuscript variant |
| Listen to the prophet (18:15 / Acts 3:22) | `אֵלָיו תִּשְׁמָעוּן` | `αυτού ακούσεσθε` | `αὐτοῦ ἀκούσεσθε` | Close Greek verbal correspondence in the inspected digital texts |
| God's words in the prophet's mouth (18:18) | `וְנָתַתִּי דְבָרַי בְּפִיו` | `και δώσω το ρήμά μου εν τω στόματι αυτού` | Acts 3:22 refers to the prophet's speaking | Deut 18:18 grounds the agency/commission motif; Acts 3:22 does not quote this full clause |
| Consequence for refusing to hear (18:19 / Acts 3:23) | `אָנֹכִי אֶדְרֹשׁ מֵעִמּוֹ` | `εγώ εκδικήσω εξ αυτού` | `ἐξολεθρευθήσεται ἐκ τοῦ λαοῦ` | **Material wording difference:** God requiring/accounting from the person versus a person being removed from the people. Test scriptural combination hypotheses, not assume a Hebrew manuscript variant |

The above alignments are **working observations** from identified transcriptions. They do not prove the textual priority of a witness, and do not yet establish whether Acts 3:23 draws on Lev 23:29, Num 15:30–31, or a shared idiom.

## Theological relevance, deliberately limited

- **[T]** Deuteronomy portrays the prophet as a person raised up by God from Israel, who communicates words God places in his mouth.
- **[T]** In Acts 3, Peter applies the Moses promise to Jesus.
- **[I]** The passage strongly foregrounds divine commission and prophetic agency, but that does **not** by itself settle whether classical Trinitarian or Biblical Unitarian Christology best explains the entire New Testament.
- **[Q]** Does Deuteronomy's immediate context primarily envision an ongoing line of prophets, a final eschatological prophet, or both? Requires literary and Jewish reception study.
