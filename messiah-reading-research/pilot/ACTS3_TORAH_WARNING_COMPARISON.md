## Direct observations [T]

| Feature | Acts 3:23 | BTB Lev 23:29 | BTB Num 15:30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening noun phrase | `πᾶσα ψυχὴ ἥτις` | `πάσα ψυχή ήτις` | `και ψυχή ήτις` |
| Destruction verb, exactly as transcribed | `ἐξολεθρευθήσεται` | `εξολοθρευθήσεται` | `εξολοθρευθήσεται` |
| Removal formula | `ἐκ τοῦ λαοῦ` | `εκ του λαού αυτής` | `εκ του λαού αυτής` |

Leviticus shares the word meaning “every”; Numbers has an additional subject phrase after the destruction verb. The destruction verb has **ε/ο spelling differences** between these sources. These are not silently normalized and cannot yet be classified as ancient manuscript variants.

Num 15:31 includes `το ρήμα κυρίου`. Acts 3:17 explicitly mentions ignorance; 3:19 calls for repentance and turning back. These observations are compatible with, but do not prove, the contextual argument below.

## Competing hypotheses [I/Q]

Eric J. Brewer, “The Prophet like Moses and the Word of the Lord: Reassigning the Composite Citation in Acts 3.22–3,” *New Testament Studies* 70(4), 486–497, [DOI 10.1017/S0028688524000134](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688524000134), argues for Numbers rather than the customary Leviticus identification. His argument links rejection of the prophet's divine message with Numbers' deliberate rejection of God's word, and connects Numbers' intentional/unintentional distinction to Acts' mention of ignorance.

The strongest immediate objection is Leviticus' closer wording, including “every.” A broader Torah punishment formula remains another possibility. Brewer's argument is a scholarly proposal, not consensus established by this dossier. His treatment of the speeches as Lukan compositions is a methodological assumption, not evidence that Peter's exact historical wording has been recovered.

## Assessment and remaining checks

**Provisional:** Both candidates deserve testing. Shared words establish resemblance; literary context helps assess possible dependence. Neither establishes a lost Hebrew reading or settles Christology.
