# Tagging System Usage Rules — Style Guide
**Project:** Debt-Debtor Thesis of Redemption — Biblical Unitarian Theology  
**Applies to:** All chapters (1–17), all appendices (B–D), and the introduction. Project-wide binding convention.  
**Source of truth:** `INTRO.md` §I.5; this guide operationalizes that section for chapter authors.

---

## Purpose

The tagging system makes the boundary between what the biblical text states and what the argument infers auditable. The reader can verify each claim against the text and against the evidence sketch that accompanies it. Every claim in the manuscript is tagged; the tag indicates the level of textual support. Every non-explicit claim carries an evidence sketch so the user can audit the inference.

The system is conservative: when in doubt, lower the band. The cost of over-claiming (claiming `[E]` for what is actually `[I]`) is a manuscript that misrepresents the text. The cost of under-claiming is that the manuscript is more cautious than it needs to be — recoverable by raising the band with a new evidence sketch.

---

## The Three Tag Classes

### `[E]` — Explicit

**Definition:** The text of Scripture, read in its immediate context, directly states the claim. The words themselves are the claim. A reader who reads the cited passage will see the claim stated.

**Requirement:** Cite the specific passage (chapter and verse, with the translation). If a translation choice affects the claim, note the underlying Hebrew/Greek term and the translation set's range of renderings (see `06_Translation_Bias_Documentation.md`).

**Does NOT require an evidence sketch.** The sketch is unnecessary because the text states the claim.

**Examples:**
- "The death-for-sin penalty is declared in Eden" — supported by Genesis 2:17 ("in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die"). `[E]`.
- "God raised Jesus" — supported by Acts 2:24 ("God raised him up, loosing the pangs of death"). `[E]`.

### `[I-band]` — Strong Implication

**Definition:** The text and its context compel the claim, but the words themselves do not directly state it. A reader who reads the cited passage and thinks about its meaning in context will arrive at the claim as the natural reading.

**Bands (low to high):**
- `[I-low]` — weak implication; the claim is possible from the text but a competent reader might read the passage differently.
- `[I-mid]` — moderate implication; the claim is the most natural reading of the immediate text but alternative readings are not obviously foreclosed.
- `[I-high]` — strong implication; the claim is the natural reading of the immediate text, supported by parallel passages and/or the canonical context, and would be `[E]` if the text stated it in propositional form.

**Requirement:** Cite the specific passage(s) that ground the inference. **Every `[I]` claim MUST carry an evidence sketch** with three components:
1. **Evidence for** — the textual arguments supporting the claim. Each is a specific observation about the text (parallel structure, verb tense, vocabulary, immediate context, canonical parallels).
2. **Evidence against / weakening** — the textual arguments that could push back on the claim. This is not a "yes-but" hedge; it is the honest identification of textual material that does not support the claim or supports a competing reading. If there is no material against, the claim is `[E]`, not `[I]`.
3. **Band justification** — a one- or two-sentence statement of why the claim is at this band (low / mid / high) and not at another. The band justification should reference the balance of for / against material.

**Examples:**
- `[I-high]` "The resurrection is the Father's vindication of the obedient Son" — supported by the consistent attribution of the resurrection to the Father across Acts and the epistles (Acts 2:24, 32; 3:15; 4:10; 5:30; 10:40; 13:30, 37; 17:31; Romans 4:24–25; 1 Corinthians 15:15; Ephesians 1:20; Colossians 2:12; 1 Thessalonians 1:10; Hebrews 13:20; 1 Peter 1:21). Against: some texts attribute resurrection power to the Son (John 2:19; 10:17–18). The consistent attribution to the Father is explicit; the vindication-of-the-obedient-representative reading is the natural interpretation of the reversal of the cross as rejection. `[I-high]` is honest.
- `[I-mid]` "The mechanism of what passed to humanity is ontic transmission" — supported by Job 14:4, Job 15:14, Acts 17:26, the parallel structure of Romans 5:12 with the ontic reading. Against: the verses are wisdom poetry not systematic theology; the link to Adam is inferential. The ontic transmission is the most natural reading; forensic imputation is also possible. `[I-mid]`.

### `[H-band]` — Hypothesis

**Definition:** A defensible reading of the text that the text does not foreclose. The reading is supported by textual evidence but goes beyond what the text directly states or strongly implies. The reader who arrives at the reading from the text alone is reading competently; the reader who arrives at a different reading is also reading competently. The hypothesis is a judgment, not a discovery.

**Bands (low to high):**
- `[H-low]` — the reading is possible from the text but the textual evidence is thin and the reading is not the most natural one.
- `[H-mid]` — the reading is a plausible systematic inference from convergent textual evidence; not a textual assertion.
- `[H-high]` — the reading is the strongest exegesis of the immediate text and the textual evidence is convergent across the canon; would be `[I-high]` if a single passage stated it in propositional form.

**Requirement:** Same as `[I-band]` — every `[H]` claim MUST carry an evidence sketch with for / against / band justification. The evidence against for an `[H]` is typically stronger than for an `[I]`; the band reflects the strength of the for-evidence relative to the against-evidence.

**Examples:**
- `[H-mid]` "The age of accountability is around the time a child can knowingly choose to disobey" — supported by the developmental moral category in Scripture (Deuteronomy 1:39; Isaiah 7:15–16; Jonah 4:11). Against: no text states a specific age. The reading is a plausible systematic inference but not a textual assertion. `[H-mid]`.
- `[H-mid]` "The 'in Adam' / 'in Christ' relation is structural (constitutive representation) rather than volitional (chosen inclusion)" — supported by the parallel structure of Romans 5:12–21 and 1 Corinthians 15:22, 45–49. Against: the 'in Christ' relation elsewhere requires faith (John 3:16; Acts 16:31; Romans 10:9); the volitional reading has biblical material behind it. The structural reading is the strongest exegesis of the immediate Romans 5 / 1 Corinthians 15 text; the volitional reading is not foreclosed. `[H-mid]`.

## What the Tagging System Does NOT Do

- It does not assign a numerical probability. A probability assigned by the author reflects the author's training-corpus priors as much as the textual evidence. The band structure preserves what is tractable: enumerable textual evidence plus a qualitative confidence judgment.
- It does not foreclose the reader's judgment. A reader who reads the same text and arrives at a different band is not wrong; they have made a different judgment about the same evidence. The point of the band is to expose the judgment, not to settle it.
- It does not validate the claim as true. The text is the validation; the band is the author's honest assessment of the claim's relationship to the text.
- It does not extend to the secondary-source identification in the manuscript. References to the "Reformed tradition," "Chalcedonian formula," "Nicene metaphysics," and similar are identification of competing positions being explicitly engaged against — allowed by the Bible-only rule — and are not tagged as evidence claims.

## Cross-Link Conventions

Every chapter ends with an Acceptance check that includes:
1. **Word count** — the approximate prose word count, with note on whether the evidence sketches are counted.
2. **Acceptance criteria** — the outline items the chapter was supposed to satisfy (W-chapter-number, A-chapter-number, R-chapter-number, E-chapter-number prefixes from `01_Architecture_and_Outline.md`), each marked done with the section that satisfies it.
3. **Cross-links** — backward links to chapters presupposed, forward links to chapters presupposing the present chapter. Format: `**Cross-link to Chapter N** — <description>: made in §N.M (<specific section and quote>).`
4. **Verification log** (for chapters with load-bearing quotations) — list of primary quotations verified against esv.org on a specific date, with the date noted.

Chapters that introduce a new concept or claim that downstream chapters depend on should have a "Forward links" section in the body (not just in the acceptance check) so the reader can follow the argument forward.

Chapters that depend on a prior chapter's argument should have a "Backward to Chapter N" reference in the section that presupposes the prior chapter.

## Band Justification Format

The band justification is the most important sentence in each evidence sketch. It tells the reader why the claim is at this band and not at another. The format is:

> *The [for-evidence summary] makes the [claim] the [strongest / most natural / plausible] reading of the immediate text; the [against-evidence summary] pulls against. `[I-band]` / `[H-band]` is honest.*

The band justification should NOT be:
- A restatement of the claim.
- A restatement of the for-evidence alone.
- A hedge ("there is some evidence on both sides, so we tag it `[I-mid]`").
- A reference to external authority ("the church has traditionally read it this way").

The band justification SHOULD:
- Reference the specific textual material that places the claim at its band.
- Note the alternative reading(s) the against-evidence supports.
- Connect the band to the textual evidence, not to the author's confidence.

## Tagging in Evidence Sketches (the `Evidence sketch (`[band]`) ...` block)

Every `[I]` and `[H]` claim must have a clearly demarcated evidence sketch. The standard format is:

```
**[band] evidence sketch — "The <claim>"**

The claim in full form. State the claim in one or two sentences with the textual context that grounds it.

**Evidence for:** (a) <textual observation with citation>. (b) <textual observation with citation>. (c) <textual observation with citation>.

**Evidence against / weakening:** (a) <textual observation that could push back>. (b) <textual observation that could push back>. (c) <textual observation that could push back>.

**Band justification:** The [for-summary] makes the [claim] the [band-strength] reading; the [against-summary] pulls against. `[band]` is honest.
```

If the claim is `[E]`, no evidence sketch is required. State the claim with the citation; the citation IS the evidence.

---

## What Counts as a "Textual Observation" in an Evidence Sketch

A textual observation is a specific, verifiable claim about the text. Examples:
- "Romans 5:12 uses *eph' hō* (because / on the basis of which) followed by *hēmarton* (they sinned) — the structure makes the consequence prior to the individual acts."
- "1 Corinthians 15:22 uses present tense (*apothnēskousin*, 'are dying') of a state already in effect for all humanity."
- "Hosea 6:7 in the Hebrew has *kə'adam* (like Adam) — the syntax can be read as 'like Adam [the man]' or 'like men [humanity generally]'."

A textual observation is NOT:
- "Augustine reads this passage as..." (secondary source; not allowed as evidence)
- "The Reformed tradition affirms..." (secondary source; not allowed as evidence)
- "Commentators generally agree..." (secondary source; not allowed as evidence)
- "It seems to me..." (personal judgment; the band carries the judgment, not the sketch)

The Bible-only rule means every textual observation must be verifiable against the biblical text in the cited translation. If the observation requires the underlying Hebrew or Greek, cite the term and reference the translation set's range of renderings.

---

## Verification of Biblical Quotations

Every load-bearing quotation in the manuscript is verified against esv.org on the date noted in the chapter's verification log. The verification log lists:
- Primary quotations — the texts the chapter's argument depends on. Each is cited with translation and reference.
- Reference-only quotations — texts cited in the structural analysis or as parallel passages. Each is cited with translation and reference.
- Translation notes (selected) — for quotations where the translation choice affects the claim.

The verification log is part of the chapter's acceptance check; the manuscript's audit-trail discipline depends on it.

---

## Summary of Tagging Rules

1. **Every claim is tagged.** No bare claims.
2. **`[E]` for explicit text.** Cite the passage; no sketch required.
3. **`[I-band]` for strong implications.** Cite the passage(s) and carry an evidence sketch with for / against / band justification.
4. **`[H-band]` for hypotheses.** Cite the passage(s) and carry an evidence sketch with for / against / band justification.
5. **Vocabulary terms the text does not use** (e.g., "federal headship," "ontic transmission") are tagged `[I-mid]` for the vocabulary, with the underlying textual observations tagged separately. No bare `[I]` or `[H]` — every non-explicit tag carries a band.
6. **No external sources as evidence.** Secondary sources are mentioned only to identify or dismiss competing positions, never as evidence for the project's claims.
7. **No numerical probabilities.** The band is the qualitative confidence judgment.
8. **No hedge phrases.** State the claim with the band and let the evidence sketch carry the audit.
9. **Cross-link every chapter** to the chapters it presupposes and the chapters that presuppose it.
10. **Verify every load-bearing quotation** against esv.org with a date noted.
11. **When in doubt, lower the band.** `[I-mid]` recoverable; `[E]` misrepresentation is not.
