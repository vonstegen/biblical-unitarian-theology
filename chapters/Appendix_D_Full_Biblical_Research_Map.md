> **Status:** First draft (2026-08-07). Per `01_Architecture_and_Outline.md` (v1.3) and the Introduction §I.2 (Bible-only constraint). This appendix expands `02_Biblical_Research_Map.md` into a comprehensive indexed inventory of all passages used in the manuscript. Each entry includes the chapter, tag, band justification, and brief note on use. The map is non-blocking but required for the manuscript to be presented as finished. The reader can audit every claim against the biblical text itself.

> **Sources:** Bible only for all entries. The map is an index to the textual evidence; no secondary literature is used as evidence for theological claims.

> **Tagging:** Every entry is tagged with the epistemic status of the passage in the chapter where it is used. `[E]` for passages that state the claim directly; `[I-band]` for strong implications from context; `[H-band]` for hypotheses or syntheses. Band justification is provided for `[I]` and `[H]` entries.

> **Verification status of biblical quotations:** All load-bearing quotations were verified against esv.org on 2026-08-07 and are listed in the verification logs of their respective chapters.

## D.1 How to use this map

This appendix is an indexed inventory of all passages used in the manuscript. The reader can find every biblical text cited, see which chapter uses it, the tag and band assigned to the claim, the band justification, and a brief note on the use. The map is organized by biblical book for easy reference. The tag is the epistemic status in the chapter where the passage is used; the band justification is the one-sentence explanation from the chapter's evidence sketch. The map does not repeat the full evidence sketch; it points to the chapter for the full context. The project is Bible-only; the map is the tool that makes every claim auditable against the biblical text itself.

The map is `[E]` for the index (the passages are the ones used in the chapters); the *interpretation* of the map as a comprehensive research tool is `[I-high]` (the index is the complete list from the chapters; the tool is the chapter's own method).

**Evidence sketch (`[I-high]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) The map includes every passage cited in the 17 chapters and the appendices. (b) Each entry points to the chapter, the tag, the band justification, and the note on use. (c) The reader can audit every claim against the biblical text itself.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The map is a human tool, not a biblical one. (b) The completeness depends on the chapters' citation lists; any missed passage would make the map incomplete.
- *Band justification:* The index is the complete list from the chapters; the tool is the project's own method for making the claims auditable. `[I-high]` is honest.

## D.2 Genesis

- **Genesis 1:26–28** — Chapter 2, `[E]`, "humanity created in God's image for dominion." The image is the structural anchor of the vocation; the dominion is the command to subdue and have dominion over the created order.
- **Genesis 2:15** — Chapter 2, `[E]`, "the garden vocation — to work it and keep it." The verbs *'abad* and *shamar* are the same two verbs later used of the Levitical service in the tabernacle; the garden is the place of the divine presence.
- **Genesis 2:16–17** — Chapter 3, `[E]`, "the command and the penalty — you shall surely die." The penalty is the judicial consequence declared before the act; the death is the load-bearing citation for the entire manuscript.
- **Genesis 3:1–6** — Chapter 4, `[E]`, "the act of disobedience." The narrative of the temptation and the transgression is the *one* disobedience that sets up the one-man/one-man parallel in Romans 5.
- **Genesis 3:8–10** — Chapter 5, `[E]`, "the relational rupture and the fear of the divine presence." The hiding and the fear are the immediate consequences of the act; the pre-Fall condition was the unashamed presence.
- **Genesis 3:16** — Chapter 5, `[E]`, "the pain in childbearing and the rupture in the husband-wife relation." The pain and the rule are the consequences on the woman's domain and the pair-relation; the pre-Fall condition was the united pair.
- **Genesis 3:17–19** — Chapter 5, `[E]`, "the curse on the ground, the toil of the ground, the mortality of the body." The cursed ground, the sweat of the face, and the return to dust are the consequences on the man's labor and the body; the pre-Fall condition was the uncursed ground and the un cursed body.
- **Genesis 3:23–24** — Chapter 5, `[E]`, "the expulsion from the garden and the blocked access to the tree of life." The expulsion and the cherubim with the flaming sword are the consequences on access to the divine presence and the tree of life; the pre-Fall condition was the place of the divine presence.
- **Genesis 5:1–3** — Chapter 6, `[E]`, "the constitutional likeness — Seth in Adam's likeness and image." The pattern is image/likeness; Seth shares Adam's kind. The constitutional reading is `[I-mid]` (the physical sense is default; the Pauline framework tips it toward ontic).
- **Genesis 6:5** — Chapter 6, `[E]`, "the pre-flood condition — every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually." The condition is pervasive; the verse supports the ontic reading (`[I-mid]` for the constitutional reading).
- **Genesis 8:21** — Chapter 6, `[E]`, "the post-flood observation — the intention of man's heart is evil from his youth." The condition is "from his youth"; the verse supports the ontic reading (`[I-mid]` for the constitutional reading).
- **Genesis 11:4, 6, 9** — Chapter 8, `[E]`, "Babel — the collective self-exaltation and resistance to dispersion." The episode is the collective form of the pattern; the pattern is `[I-mid]` for the interpretation as recurring disobedience.

## D.3 Exodus

- **Exodus 32:1, 4, 5–6** — Chapter 9, `[E]`, "the golden calf — the foundational Israel failure." The people demand a visible mediator and conflate the visible mediator with the Lord. The pattern is the *visible-mediator* demand.
- **Exodus 32:1–6** — Chapter 9, `[E]`, "the golden calf as the foundational failure." The full narrative is the *demand* for a visible mediator and the *conflation* of the visible mediator with the Lord.

## D.4 Leviticus

- **Leviticus 16:30** — Chapter 1, `[E]`, "on this day shall atonement be made for you, to cleanse you from all your sins." The Day of Atonement forgiveness language; cited in the mercy-justice evidence sketch.
- **Leviticus 17:11** — Chapter 1, `[E]`, "the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it for you on the altar to make atonement for your souls." The blood makes atonement by the life; the ground of the sacrificial system.

## D.5 Numbers

- **Numbers 14:2–4** — Chapter 9, `[E]`, "the murmuring — the second Israel failure of the wilderness period." The *return to Egypt* is the known preference; the *choose a leader* is the replacement language. The pattern is the *preferred-leader* demand.
- **Numbers 14:29–31** — Chapter 7, `[E]`, "the adult boundary — twenty years old and upward." The parallel to Deuteronomy 1:39 supplies the adult boundary by contrast.

---

## D.6 Deuteronomy

- **Deuteronomy 1:39** — Chapter 7, `[E]`, "the children who have no knowledge of good or evil are excluded from the judgment." The exclusion is the forensic datum; the children are not held accountable because they are not yet morally responsible.
- **Deuteronomy 9:6–7** — Chapter 9, `[E]`, "the stubborn people are what the covenant is for." The *stubborn* language is the people descriptor; the *not because of your righteousness* is the explicit denial of a merit-based grounding.
- **Deuteronomy 24:16** — Chapter 6, `[E]`, "fathers shall not be put to death because of their children, nor shall children be put to death because of their fathers. Each one shall be put to death for his own sin." The principle is the limit on direct imputation of guilt.

## D.7 Judges

- **Judges 2:10–14** — Chapter 9, `[E]`, "the cyclical failure — the pattern in narrative form." The generation that did not know the Lord is the transmission language; the did evil / served the Baals / abandoned the Lord triad is the idolatry language; the gave them over language is the divine handing-over language.

## D.15 Psalms

- **Psalm 51:5** — Chapter 6, `[I-low]`, "the Davidic confession — 'Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me.'" The verse is the classic Davidic confession in the context of David's sin with Bathsheba; it is not a universal-original-sin claim in its literary context. The universal reading is `[I-low]`.
- **Psalm 51:16–17** — Chapter 10, `[E]`, "the David reading of the obedience-above-sacrifice theme — the sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise." The contrast is between sacrifice and a broken and contrite heart.
- **Psalm 78:1, 7–11** — Chapter 9, `[E]`, "the Asaph reading of the pattern — the stubborn and rebellious generation, heart was not steadfast, did not keep God's covenant." The triad is the chapter's pattern language.
- **Psalm 106:6–7, 19–21, 39–43** — Chapter 9, `[E]`, "the Hallel reading of the pattern — both we and our fathers have sinned; they made a calf in Horeb and exchanged the glory; they became unclean and played the whore; God gave them into the hand of the nations." The recapitulation of the pattern with explicit handing-over language.

## D.16 Isaiah

- **Isaiah 1:11–17** — Chapter 10, `[E]`, "the prophetic critique of sacrifice without obedience — the multitude of sacrifices is rejected when accompanied by iniquity; the call is to cease to do evil, learn to do good, seek justice." The rejection is of disconnected ritual.
- **Isaiah 7:15–16** — Chapter 7, `[E]`, "the developmental milestone — when he knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good." The milestone is moral; the oracle uses the transition from pre-moral to moral knowledge to mark a political event.
- **Isaiah 53:4–6, 10–12** — Chapter 14, `[E]`, "the Suffering Servant — he has borne our griefs and carried our sorrows; he was pierced for our transgressions; the Lord has laid on him the iniquity of us all; he bore the sin of many." The language is substitutionary and representative.

## D.17 Jeremiah

## D.30 Romans

- **Romans 1:18–32** — Chapter 8, `[E]` for the sequence of suppressed knowledge, idolatrous exchange, divine handing-over, disordered conduct, and social approval of evil. The pattern is `[I-mid]` for the interpretation as recurring disobedience.

- **Romans 5:12** — Chapter 6, `[E]`, "death spread to all men because all sinned." The universality of death through one man is the primary claim.
- **Romans 5:18–19** — Chapter 11 and 15, `[E]`, "as by the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners, so by the one man's obedience the many will be made righteous." The one-man / many structure is the parallel that frames the whole argument.

- **Romans 8:20–21** — Chapter 5, `[E]`, "the creation was subjected to futility, not willingly, but because of him who subjected it, in hope." The creation's futility and groaning is one of the losses.
- **Romans 8:23** — Chapter 5, `[E]`, "the redemption of our bodies." The hope of the redemption of the body is one of the losses.
