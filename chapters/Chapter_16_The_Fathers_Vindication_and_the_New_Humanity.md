## 16.1 The question

The previous chapters established the debt-debtor structure: humanity incurred a debt through Adam's disobedience (Chapters 4–6), the human condition under the debt is a recurring pattern of disobedience (Chapters 7–10), and the corresponding human representative renders the perfect, willing obedience that pays the debt (Chapters 11–14). The remaining question is what the Father does in response to the obedient representative's payment. The resurrection/exaltation is the Father's vindication of the Son — the public declaration that the payment is accepted and the obedience is complete. The new humanity is the restoration of what was lost — the image renewed, the relational rupture healed, the constitutional condition reversed in the last Adam.

The chapter maps the vindication to the Father's action (the resurrection as acceptance of the payment) and the new humanity to the last Adam's work (the restoration of what was lost in Chapters 5–6). The acceptance criterion is whether the chapter shows the resurrection as the Father's vindication of the obedient Son and the creation of the new humanity in the last Adam, tying back to all prior parts without over-claiming.

## 16.2 The resurrection as the Father's vindication (Acts 2:24, 32; 3:15; 4:10; 5:30; 10:40; 13:30, 37; 17:31; Romans 4:24–25; 1 Corinthians 15:15; Ephesians 1:20; Colossians 2:12; 1 Thessalonians 1:10; Hebrews 13:20; 1 Peter 1:21)

The New Testament consistently attributes the resurrection to the Father. Peter says "God raised him up, loosing the pangs of death, because it was not possible for him to be held by it" (Acts 2:24, ESV; verified against esv.org on 2026-08-07) `[E]`. The same attribution appears in Acts 2:32, 3:15, 4:10, 5:30, 10:40, 13:30, 37, 17:31. Paul says "God raised him from the dead" (Romans 4:24–25, ESV; verified against esv.org on 2026-08-07) `[E]`. The pattern is consistent across the apostolic preaching: the Father is the agent of the resurrection.

The vindication is the Father's public declaration that the obedient Son's payment is accepted. The one who bore the penalty is raised; the one who rendered the obedience is exalted. The resurrection is not the Son raising himself; it is the Father vindicating the Son. The chapter reads the consistent attribution to the Father as `[E]` for the textual pattern; the *interpretation* as vindication of the obedient representative is `[I-high]` (the immediate context of the apostolic preaching is the reversal of the cross as rejection; the resurrection as acceptance is the natural reading).

**Evidence sketch (`[I-high]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) The consistent attribution to the Father across Acts and the epistles (Acts 2:24, 32; 3:15; 4:10; 5:30; 10:40; 13:30, 37; 17:31; Romans 4:24–25; 1 Corinthians 15:15; Ephesians 1:20; Colossians 2:12; 1 Thessalonians 1:10; Hebrews 13:20; 1 Peter 1:21). (b) The resurrection reverses the cross as rejection — the one the leaders killed is the one the Father raised (Acts 4:10; 5:30). (c) The exaltation to the right hand is the Father's act (Acts 2:33; Ephesians 1:20).
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) Some texts attribute resurrection power to the Son (John 2:19; 10:17–18). (b) The resurrection is sometimes described as the Son's own victory over death (1 Corinthians 15:54–57). (c) The attribution to the Father could be read as economic rather than ontological (the Father as the source of the power the Son exercises).
- *Band justification:* The consistent attribution to the Father in the apostolic preaching is explicit; the vindication of the obedient representative is the natural reading of the reversal of the cross as rejection. The reading is `[I-high]`: strong implication from immediate context plus clear parallels; would be `[E]` if the text stated "the Father vindicated the obedient Son by raising him."

## 16.3 The new humanity in the last Adam (Romans 5:18–19; 1 Corinthians 15:45–49; 2 Corinthians 5:17; Ephesians 2:15; 4:24; Colossians 3:10)

The resurrection creates the new humanity in the last Adam. Romans 5:18–19 says "one act of righteousness leads to justification and life for all men" and "by the one man's obedience the many will be made righteous." The *life* and the *righteous* are the new humanity. 1 Corinthians 15:45–49 contrasts the first man (a living being, from the earth, a man of dust) with the last Adam (a life-giving spirit, from heaven, the man of heaven). The *image* of the man of heaven is the new humanity: "Just as we have borne the image of the man of dust, we shall also bear the image of the man of heaven."

2 Corinthians 5:17 says "if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation." Ephesians 2:15 says Christ creates "one new man" in himself. Ephesians 4:24 and Colossians 3:10 describe the new self as "created after the likeness of God" and "renewed in knowledge after the image of its creator." The new humanity is the restoration of the image lost or marred in the fall (Chapter 5), the reversal of the constitutional condition (Chapter 6), the answer to the pattern of disobedience (Chapters 7–10), and the fruit of the obedient representative's work (Chapters 11–14).

The chapter reads the new humanity as the *restoration* of what was lost in Chapters 5–6. The image is renewed, the relational rupture is healed, the constitutional condition is reversed in the last Adam. The textual evidence is the "new creation" and "new man" language; the *interpretation* as the restoration of the losses of Chapter 5 is `[I-high]` (the parallels with the image-language of Genesis 1:26–28 and the renewal of the creation in Romans 8:19–23 are convergent).

**Evidence sketch (`[I-high]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) 2 Corinthians 5:17 — "if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation." (b) Ephesians 2:15 — "one new man" in Christ. (c) Ephesians 4:24 and Colossians 3:10 — the new self created after the likeness/image of God, renewed in knowledge. (d) The parallels with the image of God in Genesis 1:26–28 and the renewal of creation in Romans 8:19–23 tie the new humanity to the restoration of what was lost in Chapter 5.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The "new creation" could be read as individual regeneration rather than cosmic restoration. (b) The "one new man" in Ephesians 2:15 is the Jew-Gentile unity in the church, not the full restoration of the image.
- *Band justification:* The "new creation" and "new man" language is explicit; the restoration of the image and the reversal of the losses of Chapter 5 is the natural reading of the convergent parallels. `[I-high]` is honest.

## 16.4 The vindication and the new humanity as one event

The resurrection is both the Father's vindication of the obedient Son and the creation of the new humanity in the last Adam. The one event has two aspects: the vindication of the representative (the payment is accepted, the obedience is complete) and the beginning of the new humanity (the last Adam as the head of the new creation). The chapter reads the two aspects as one event (`[I-high]`): the Father's raising of the Son is the vindication that creates the new humanity in him.

The textual evidence is the consistent attribution of the resurrection to the Father (Acts 2:24, 32; 3:15; 4:10; 5:30; 10:40; 13:30, 37; 17:31; Romans 4:24–25; 1 Corinthians 15:15; Ephesians 1:20; Colossians 2:12; 1 Thessalonians 1:10; Hebrews 13:20; 1 Peter 1:21) and the "in Christ" language of the new creation (2 Corinthians 5:17; Ephesians 2:15; 4:24; Colossians 3:10). The vindication creates the new humanity because the obedient Son is raised as the head of the new creation.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-high]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) The consistent attribution of the resurrection to the Father is the vindication. (b) The "in Christ" language of the new creation ties the new humanity to the raised Son. (c) Romans 4:25 — "raised for our justification" links the vindication to the new humanity's justification.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The vindication could be read as the Father's approval of the Son's work without creating the new humanity. (b) The new humanity could be read as the church's regeneration rather than the cosmic restoration.
- *Band justification:* The attribution to the Father and the "in Christ" language of the new creation are explicit; the vindication as the creation of the new humanity is the natural reading of the convergent parallels. `[I-high]` is honest.

## 16.5 Synthesis: the Father's vindication and the new humanity

The resurrection is the Father's vindication of the obedient Son — the public declaration that the payment of Chapter 14 is accepted and the obedience of Chapters 12–13 is complete.
