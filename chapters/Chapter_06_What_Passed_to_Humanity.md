## 6.1 The question

The chapter's question is the *progenitive* consequence: what *passes* from Adam to his descendants as the result of his act? The question is distinct from Chapter 5's losses (which are the *immediate* consequences on Adam and the creation). The progenitive consequence is the *transmission* — the *kind* of way the consequence of Adam's act propagates to the descendants.

The chapter's *acceptance criterion* is explicit: the *death* that passed to all is `[E]`; the *mechanism* by which guilt or sin pass is `[I]` or `[H]`. The chapter does not stake the main thesis on the *mechanism* question. The text is more explicit on the *fact* of transmission than on the *mechanism* of transmission.

The chapter distinguishes three possible positions on the *mechanism*:

1. **Imputation of sin** — Adam's sin is *accounted* to his descendants as *their* sin. The mechanism is forensic-juridical (the *reckoning* of Adam's sin to the descendants as a juridical matter).
2. **Inherited corruption** — Adam's descendants *inherit* a corrupted condition (the disordered nature) which is itself the *kind* of being that sin can occur in. The mechanism is ontic (the descendants *are* the kind of being that sin is natural for).
3. **Both** — the *forensic* imputation and the *ontic* corruption are both present. The mechanism is the convergence of the two.

The chapter's task is to *enumerate* the textual evidence, *tag* each claim with the appropriate band, and *refrain* from over-claiming on the *mechanism* question. The chapter's argument does not depend on the resolution of the *mechanism* question.

## 6.2 The death that passed to all (Romans 5:12)

The Pauline text is the explicit textual basis for the *progenitive* consequence that the chapter foregrounds.

> "Therefore, just as sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all men because all sinned" (Romans 5:12, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is the *load-bearing* text. Three features are stated:

**First, the death is universal.** "Death spread to all men." The universality is *structural*, not selective. The chapter treats this as the *explicit* textual evidence that the *death* is the *progenitive* consequence propagated to all humanity. `[E]`

**Second, the mechanism is "because all sinned."** The Greek *ἐφ᾽ ᾧ πάντες ἥμαρτον* (*eph' hō pantes hēmarton*) is grammatically ambiguous. The RSV / NASB / ESV render it "because all sinned" (taking *eph' hō* as the causal conjunction). The KJV / NKJV render it "for that all sinned" (taking *eph' hō* as a relative pronoun referring to the *spread* of death). The substantive meaning is the same: the universality of death is connected to the universality of sin. The grammatical question is whether the *sin* is the *cause* of the *death* or the *result* of the *death* (or of being *in Adam*). The chapter treats the *fact* of the connection as `[E]` and the *direction* of the connection as `[I-mid]` (see evidence sketch in §6.6).

**Third, the death is "through one man."** The *one man* is the *Adam* of the preceding verses (and is identified as such in 1 Corinthians 15:21–22). The death is *through* the one man — the mechanism is *through* Adam's act, not (immediately) through each individual's own sin. The universality of death is *consequent* on Adam's act; the universality of sin is *consequent* on the fact that all are *in Adam*. Both are universal because of the *one man*. The chapter treats this as `[E]` for the textual structure.

The chapter's *primary* claim — the death passed to all — is `[E]`. The chapter's *secondary* claim — the mechanism by which the death is passed — is `[I-mid]` or `[H-mid]`. The acceptance criterion is honored: the *death* is asserted with strong textual basis; the *mechanism* is acknowledged as a separate question.

## 6.3 The "in Adam" structure (1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49)

The Corinthians parallel adds the *in Adam* vocabulary that the rest of the chapter presupposes.

> "For as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive." (1 Corinthians 15:21–22, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The parallel structure is *en Adamō / en Christō* — *in* Adam / *in* Christ. The *in* is the same on both sides; the *en* (Greek preposition) does not change between the two clauses. The chapter treats the *en* as a *representative* relation — the *in* is the *sphere* of the representative's act. Being *in Adam* is to be within the sphere of Adam's act; being *in Christ* is to be within the sphere of Christ's act. The *in* is not *geographic* (Adam and Christ are not *places*) but *representative* (the *in* is the *constitutive* relation to the representative). `[E]` for the textual structure; the *interpretation* of *en* as *representative* rather than *organic* or *geographic* is `[I-high]` (see evidence sketch in Chapter 15 §15.10).

The Corinthians text also adds the *As* / *so also* parallel structure. The *hōsper* / *houtōs kai* ("as ... so also") is the *parallel* construction. The two acts are *parallel* in their *kind* (one-act / many-effect): "as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection." The parallel is *type-typified* — Adam is the *type* of the one who was to come (Romans 5:14). The chapter treats the parallel as `[E]` for the textual structure; the *interpretation* as *type-typified* is `[I-high]`.

## 6.4 The Genesis 5:1–3 evidence

The Genesis genealogy contains the textual evidence that the *constitution* of Adam's offspring is *in* Adam's *constitution*.

> "When God created man, he made him in the likeness of God." (Genesis 5:1, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

> "When Adam had lived 130 years, he fathered a son in his own likeness, after his image, and named him Seth." (Genesis 5:3, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The two verses are *paired*. Genesis 5:1 states that Adam was made in the *likeness of God*. Genesis 5:3 states that Seth was made in Adam's *likeness after his image*. The textual pattern is *image/likeness* — Seth is the *kind* of being Adam is, in the *constitutional* sense. The chapter reads the *likeness* as *constitutional* (the kind of being Adam is, the kind of being Seth is too). The reading is `[I-high]` (the textual evidence is real; the *interpretation* as *constitutional* rather than *physical* relies on the Pauline framework).

The chapter treats the *likeness* as the *ontic* (the kind of being) basis for the *transmission* of the consequence. The *transmission* is *constitutive* — Adam's descendants are *in* Adam's constitution, and the *consequence* of Adam's act propagates by the *kind* of being the descendants are. The chapter reads the *constitution* as the *basis* of the *transmission* without specifying the *mechanism* (whether the transmission is *imputation* or *inherited corruption* or *both*). The chapter's argument is the *constitution* itself — the *kind* of being Adam's descendants are — supports the *transmission* without resolving the *mechanism* question.

## 6.5 The Genesis 6:5 evidence

The Genesis 6:5 passage is the explicit textual evidence for the *intensification* of the consequence in the post-Fall generation.

> "The Lord saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually." (Genesis 6:5, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is post-Fall (the chapter is the *pre-Flood* narrative). The verse describes the *condition* of humanity as "every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually." The *condition* is the *post-Fall* constitution — the *kind* of being the descendants are. The chapter reads the verse as the *post-Fall* reading of the *constitutional* transmission: the descendants are *constitutionally* in the *bent* direction (the *evil continually* language). The reading is `[I-high]` (the textual evidence is the *language of the verse*; the *interpretation* as the *constitutional* transmission of the bent direction is the Pauline framework).

The chapter also notes the *parallel* with Genesis 8:21: "the intention of man's heart is evil from his youth" (ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`. The *from his youth* language is the *developmental* reading of the *constitution* — the *bent* direction is *present* from the earliest developmental stage. The chapter reads this as *developmental confirmation* of the *constitutional* transmission — the *bent* direction is *present* at the beginning of the human's life, not acquired through life-experience. `[E]` for the textual parallel; the *interpretation* as *developmental confirmation* is `[I-high]`.

## 6.6 The Romans 5:13–14 evidence

The Pauline text extends the *constitutional* transmission to the period *before* the Mosaic law.

> "For sin indeed was in the world before the law was given, but sin is not counted where there is no law. Yet death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those whose sinning was not like the transgression of Adam, who was a type of the one who was to come." (Romans 5:13–14, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse establishes two points:

**First, death reigned from Adam to Moses.** The *reign* of death is the *constitutional* condition — even during the period when the *law* was not given, death was *operative*. The death is *not* contingent on the *commandment* (which was given at Sinai); the death is *operative* from Adam to Moses and from Moses onward. The continuity is the *constitutional* transmission. `[E]`

**Second, Adam was a type of the one who was to come.** The *type* relation is the *parallel* the chapter presupposes — Adam is the *type* of Christ (developed in Chapter 15). The *type* is *representative* — the one man's act has *constitutive* effect on the many. The chapter treats the *type* relation as `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as *representative* rather than *biological* is `[I-high]`.

The chapter also notes the *specific* feature of the verse: "even over those whose sinning was not like the transgression of Adam." The *not like* language is the *counter-example* — those who did not themselves *transgress* in the manner of Adam (i.e., they did not *consciously* eat the fruit) still *died*. The death is *not* contingent on the *individual* transgression; the death is *constitutive* of the *in Adam* condition. `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as *constitutional transmission* is `[I-high]`.

**`[I-mid]` evidence sketch — "The mechanism of death's transmission is imputation of sin to Adam's descendants"**

- *Evidence for:* (a) The Pauline corporatism (Romans 5:12–21; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22) treats Adam's act as the *constitutive* act for the descendants; the *in* Adam is the *forensic* membership in Adam's act. (b) The Reformed orthodox tradition (the *covenant of works* / *federal headship* framework) has consistently read the *in* as the *forensic* relation; the *Westminster Confession of Faith* and the *Three Forms of Unity* both treat Adam's sin as the *imputed* sin of the descendants. (c) The *judgment* language of Romans 5:16 ("the judgment following one trespass brought condemnation") is judicial-forensic; the *condemnation* is the *forensic* consequence of Adam's trespass extended to the descendants.
- *Evidence against:* (a) The *imputation* language is *not* in the text itself; the *technical* vocabulary of *imputation* is a systematic-theological synthesis. (b) The Pauline *imputation* language is sometimes attached to *Christ* (2 Corinthians 5:21 — "made to be sin for us") rather than to *Adam*; the *imputation* of Adam's sin to the descendants is a *parallel* construction that the systematic tradition has built. (c) The *ontic* reading (Adam's descendants *are* in the constitution Adam is; the *bent* direction is the *kind* of being they are) does not require the *forensic* imputation to operate; the *constitutional* transmission is sufficient to explain the death.
- *Band justification:* The *textual* evidence supports the *constitutional* transmission (`[E]`); the *interpretation* of the *constitutional* transmission as *forensic* imputation is the systematic-theological synthesis that the chapter treats as `[I-mid]`. The chapter does not stake the main argument on the imputation reading.

## 6.7 The Josephus-direction: every man is "in Adam" by descent (Acts 17:26)

The Acts passage is the *ethnographic* confirmation of the *in Adam* relation.

> "And he made from one man every nation of mankind to live on all the face of the earth, having determined allotted periods and the boundaries of their dwelling place" (Acts 17:26, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is the Areopagus sermon — Paul's address to the Athenian philosophers. The *one man* (Greek *henos*) is *Adam* (the Greek *henos* is the same word used in Romans 5:12 for the *one man* through whom sin came). The *every nation* is the *all* of the *one man*. The chapter reads the *one man* as the *ethnographic* confirmation of the *constitutional* transmission — every human being is *constitutionally* in the *one man* by *descent*. The reading is `[I-high]` (the textual evidence is the *one man* language; the *interpretation* as the *ethnographic* confirmation of the *constitutional* transmission relies on the Pauline framework).

## 6.8 The Ezekiel 18:4 and 18:20 evidence: individual accountability

The chapter now turns to the *counter-evidence* — the Ezekiel passages that *limit* the *constitutional* transmission. The chapter explicitly defers the *individual accountability* question to Chapter 9 (where it is contextually developed); the *acknowledgment* of the *limit* is the *deferral*.

> "Behold, all souls are mine; the soul of the father as well as the soul of the son is mine: the soul who sins shall die." (Ezekiel 18:4, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

> "The soul who sins shall die. The son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father, nor the father suffer for the iniquity of the son. The righteousness of the righteous shall be upon himself, and the wickedness of the wicked shall be upon himself." (Ezekiel 18:20, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The Ezekiel passage is explicit: the *son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father*. The text is *juridical* — the son is *not* held accountable for the father's sin. The chapter reads this as the *limit* on the *constitutional* transmission: the descendants are *in* Adam's constitution, but each individual is *also* accountable for *their own* sin. The two are *not* contradictory; the *constitutional* transmission is the *kind* of being the descendants are, and the *individual* accountability is the *forensic* judgment that each individual *also* faces for their own sin.

The chapter treats the Ezekiel passages as `[E]` for the *individual* accountability. The relationship between the *constitutional* transmission (the *in* Adam condition) and the *individual* accountability (the *son shall not suffer for the father*) is developed in Chapter 9 (where the *individual* accountability is the *Ezekiel 18 framework* and the *constitutional* transmission is the *Pauline framework*). For the present chapter, the *Ezekiel 18 limit* is acknowledged but not developed — the chapter's argument is the *constitutional* transmission, and the *individual* accountability is the *limit* that the *constitutional* transmission does not abrogate.

**`[I-mid]` evidence sketch — "The Ezekiel 18:20 limit is consistent with the Pauline constitutional transmission"**

- *Evidence for:* (a) The two passages address *different* questions — Ezekiel 18:20 addresses the *forensic* judgment ("the son shall not suffer for the *iniquity* of the father"); the Pauline passages address the *constitutional* condition ("in Adam all die"). The *forensic* judgment of the *son* for the *father's* sin is excluded; the *constitutional* transmission of the *bent* direction is *not* excluded. (b) The *literary context* of Ezekiel 18 is the *prophetic* correction of the *proverb* "the fathers have eaten sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge" (Ezekiel 18:2) — the *proverb* is the *exaggeration* of the *constitutional* transmission into a *forensic* imputation; the *correction* is that the *forensic* imputation is *not* the model's framework. (c) The *Jeremiah 31:29–30* parallel (cited in §6.9) makes the *same* distinction: the *proverb* is rejected ("the fathers have eaten sour grapes ... shall no longer be said"); the *individual* accountability is affirmed ("everyone shall die for his own iniquity").
- *Evidence against:* (a) The reading of *constitutional* transmission as *not forensically* imputing the father's sin is a *distinction* that the Ezekiel text does not explicitly make. (b) The *constitutional* transmission of the *bent* direction is the *kind* of being the descendants are; the *individual* sin is *consequent* on the *constitutional* bent direction. The two are *conjoined* — the *bent* direction leads to the *individual* sin; the *individual* sin is *not* the *forensic* imputation of the father's sin. (c) The Eastern Orthodox tradition (and some Reformed theologians) read the *constitutional* transmission as *real* original sin — the *bent* direction is *forensic* liability as well as *ontic* corruption. The *Ezekiel 18:20 limit* is interpreted as *this-worldly* judgment (the *legal* judgment) and not as the *forensic* imputation of the *constitutional* sin.
- *Band justification:* The *literary context* of Ezekiel 18 (the *proverb* correction) supports the *distinction* between the *constitutional* transmission and the *forensic* imputation. The Pauline and Ezekiel passages are *compatible* once the *constitutional* / *forensic* distinction is made. The chapter treats the *compatibility* as `[I-mid]` because the *distinction* is a systematic-theological synthesis that the text itself does not explicitly draw.

## 6.9 The Jeremiah 31:29–30 evidence

The Jeremiah passage is the *parallel* to Ezekiel 18 — the *proverb* is rejected; the *individual* accountability is affirmed.

> "In those days they shall no longer say: 'The fathers have eaten sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge.' But everyone shall die for his own iniquity. Each man who eats sour grapes, his teeth shall be set on edge." (Jeremiah 31:29–30, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The literary pattern is the same as Ezekiel 18 — the *proverb* is rejected; the *individual* accountability is affirmed. The chapter reads the *proverb* as the *exaggeration* of the *constitutional* transmission into a *forensic* imputation; the *correction* is that the *forensic* imputation is *not* the model's framework. The chapter treats the *individual* accountability as the *limit* on the *constitutional* transmission: the *constitutional* transmission is the *kind* of being the descendants are; the *individual* accountability is the *forensic* judgment that each individual *also* faces for their own sin. The two are not contradictory; the *constitutional* transmission is the *constitutional* fact, and the *individual* accountability is the *forensic* fact. `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *literary* correction of the *forensic* imputation is `[I-high]`.

## 6.10 The Psalm 51:5 evidence

The Psalm 51:5 passage is the *classic* Davidic confession. The chapter has discussed the verse in Chapter 4 §4.10 translation note 1; the chapter treats the verse as the *referenced* but not *load-bearing* text. The chapter reproduces the *classification* from Chapter 4: the verse is the *Davidic confession* in the context of David's sin with Bathsheba; the verse is *not* a *universal-original-sin* claim in its literary context.

> "Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me." (Psalm 51:5, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The chapter treats the verse as `[I]` at best — the *Davidic confession* is *real* ("I was brought forth in iniquity") but the *interpretation* as the *universal-original-sin* claim is the systematic-theological synthesis (the Augustinian / Reformed reading) that the chapter does not endorse. The chapter notes that the *constitutional* transmission is the *kind* of being the descendants are; the *Davidic confession* is the *kind* of being *David* is, and David is using the *kind* of being he is as the *kind* of being the *transgression* is — the *kind* of being that *sin* is *natural* for. The reading is `[I]` (`[I]` at best; the chapter rates the verse as *not load-bearing* for the *constitutional* transmission argument).

The chapter treats the *Psalm 51:5* textual evidence as the *secondary* evidence for the *constitutional* transmission. The *primary* evidence is the Pauline corporatism (Romans 5:12–21; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22); the *Psalm 51:5* is the *supporting* evidence that the *constitutional* bent direction is the *kind* of being the descendants are. The chapter *defers* the *Psalm 51:5* reading to the *secondary*; the *primary* argument is the Pauline corporatism.

## 6.11 The Ephesians 2:1–3 evidence

The Ephesians passage is the *post-redemption* reading of the *pre-redemption* condition.

> "And you were dead in the trespasses and sins in which you once walked, following the course of this world, following the prince of the power of the air, the spirit that is now at work in the sons of disobedience — among whom we all once lived in the passions of our flesh, carrying out the desires of the flesh and the mind, and were by nature children of wrath, like the rest of mankind." (Ephesians 2:1–3, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is the *post-redemption* reading of the *pre-redemption* condition. The *dead* in the *trespasses and sins* is the *constitutional* condition — the *kind* of being the descendants are *in Adam*. The *children of wrath* (Greek *teknia orgēs*) is the *constitutional* description — the *kind* of being the descendants are is the *kind* of being the *wrath* is *natural* for. The reading is `[I-high]` (the textual evidence is the language of the verse; the *interpretation* as the *constitutional* transmission is the Pauline framework).

The chapter also notes the *parallel* in Ephesians 2:5 — "even when we were dead in our trespasses, made us alive together with Christ" (ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`. The *were dead* is the *constitutional* condition; the *made us alive* is the *constitutional* restoration. The two are the *constitutional* pair — the *constitutional* transmission of the *dead* condition is the *pre-redemption* state; the *constitutional* restoration to the *alive* condition is the *post-redemption* state. The chapter treats the *alive* condition as the *forward* reference to Chapter 16 (the *redemption of the bodies*). `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *constitutional* pair is `[I-high]`.

## 6.12 The Job 14:4 and Job 15:14–16 evidence

The Job passages are the *wisdom* reflection on the *constitutional* condition.

> "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? There is not one." (Job 14:4, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

> "What is man, that he can be pure? Or he who is born of a woman, that he can be righteous? Behold, God puts no trust in his holy ones, and the heavens are not pure in his sight; how much less one who is abominable and corrupt, a man who drinks injustice like water!" (Job 15:14–16, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The Job passages are the *wisdom* reflection on the *kind* of being the descendants are. The verse *is not* the *original-sin* claim in the *Reformed* sense; the verse is the *wisdom* observation that the *kind* of being the descendants are is the *kind* of being that *evil* is *natural* for. The chapter reads the verses as *supporting* the *constitutional* transmission: the *unclean* is the *kind* of being the descendants are; the *clean* cannot be brought out of the *unclean* except by *intervention*. The chapter treats the *intervention* as the *forward* reference to Chapter 11 (the *Last Adam* who is the *kind* of being the *clean* is). `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *constitutional* transmission is `[I-high]`.

## 6.13 The "death is the wage of sin" synthesis (Romans 6:23)

The Romans 6:23 verse is the *synthesis* of the *constitutional* transmission and the *individual* accountability.

> "For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." (Romans 6:23, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is the *existential* statement of the *constitutional* condition. The *wages of sin* is the *constitutional* consequence of the *individual* sin (the *forensic* dimension); the *free gift* is the *constitutional* restoration (the *forward* reference to Chapter 16). The chapter treats the verse as the *synthesis* of the *constitutional* transmission and the *individual* accountability: the *constitutional* transmission is the *constitutional* fact; the *individual* accountability is the *forensic* fact; the *wage* is the *consequence* of the individual sin; the *free gift* is the *restoration* of the *constitutional* condition. `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *synthesis* is `[I-high]`.

## 6.14 The chapter's argument in summary

The chapter's *primary* claim is the *death that passed to all*. The textual evidence is the Pauline corporatism (Romans 5:12–21; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49), the Genesis 5:1–3 *in* Adam/*in* his own likeness pattern, the Genesis 6:5 *bent* direction language, and the Acts 17:26 *one man* / *every nation* language. The chapter rates the *death* as `[E]`.

The chapter's *secondary* claim is the *constitutional* transmission of the *bent* direction. The textual evidence is the *in Adam* structure, the Genesis 5:3 *in his own likeness* language, the Genesis 6:5 *evil continually* language, the Genesis 8:21 *from his youth* language, the Job 14:4 *unclean* / *clean* language, the Job 15:14–16 *abominable and corrupt* language, and the Ephesians 2:1–3 *dead in trespasses and sins* language. The chapter rates the *constitutional* transmission as `[I-high]`.

The chapter's *limit* on the *constitutional* transmission is the *individual* accountability. The textual evidence is the Ezekiel 18:4 and 18:20 *son shall not suffer for the father* language, the Jeremiah 31:29–30 *individual* accountability, and the Deuteronomy 24:16 *each one shall die for his own sin* language. The chapter treats the *limit* as `[E]` for the *individual* accountability.

The chapter's *mechanism* question is *deferred*. The *mechanism* is the *imputation* of Adam's sin (*forensic-juridical*), the *inherited corruption* of the human condition (*ontic*), or *both*. The chapter does not stake the argument on the *mechanism* question. The chapter rates the *imputation* reading as `[I-mid]` and the *inherited corruption* reading as `[I-high]`.

The chapter's *forward* references are the *individual* accountability (developed in Chapter 9), the *constitutional* restoration in Christ (developed in Chapter 16), and the *kind* of intervention that addresses the *constitutional* transmission (developed in Chapters 11–14). The chapter's argument is the *kind* of being the descendants are; the *intervention* is the *restoration* of the *kind* of being the descendants are to be.

**Translation notes:**

1. **Romans 5:12** "because all sinned" (Greek *ἐφ᾽ ᾧ πάντες ἥμαρτον*): the *causal* reading is the *traditional* patristic and Reformed reading (the *sin* is the *cause* of the *death*); the *consecutive* reading is the *Arminian* and *Eastern Orthodox* reading (the *sin* is the *consequence* of the *death* or of the *in Adam* condition). The chapter treats the *direction* of the connection as `[I-mid]` (the textual evidence supports both readings; the *direction* depends on the prior commitment to the *imputation* or the *inherited-corruption* reading).

2. **Genesis 5:3** "in his own likeness, after his image" (Hebrew *bədəmût*, *bəṣaləmô*): the *constitutional* reading (the descendants inherit Adam's *constitution*) is the *Reformed* / *federal-headship* reading. The *physical* reading (the descendants resemble Adam physically) is the *natural* reading. The chapter reads the *likeness* as *constitutional* consistent with the Pauline framework; the reading is `[I-high]`.

3. **Ezekiel 18:20** "The son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father": the *son* is *not* held *forensicly* accountable for the *father's* sin. The *son* is *constitutionally* in the *kind* of being the father is (the *in Adam* condition); the *son* is *individually* accountable for *his own* sin. The two are *distinct* (constitutional and forensic); the chapter treats the *distinction* as `[I-mid]`.

4. **Romans 6:23** "the wages of sin is death" (Greek *ὀψώνιον* / *thanatos*): the *wages* (*opsonion*) is the *military pay* metaphor — the *forensic* reward that sin pays to the *constitutional* condition. The *free gift* (*xarisma*) is the *constitutional* restoration that the *intervention* brings. The chapter treats the *military* metaphor as the *forensic* parallel to the *constitutional* transmission.
