## 6.1 The question

Adam's act had immediate consequences on Adam and the creation (Chapter 5). This chapter asks the *progenitive* question: what propagates further — what passes from Adam to his descendants as the consequence of his act? The question is distinct from Chapter 5's losses, which were the *immediate* consequences. The progenitive consequence is the *transmission* — the way the consequence of Adam's act extends to the descendants.

Three positions on the mechanism of transmission are possible:

1. **Imputation of sin** — Adam's sin is accounted to his descendants as their sin; the mechanism is forensic-juridical.
2. **Inherited corruption** — Adam's descendants inherit a corrupted condition (the disordered nature) which is itself the kind of being in which sin occurs; the mechanism is ontic.
3. **Both** — forensic imputation and ontic corruption are both present.

The chapter's *primary* claim is that *death passed to all*; this is `[E]`. The chapter commits to the *ontic* mechanism at `[I-high]` (death propagates because descendants *are* the kind of being Adam's act produced). Whether a *forensic imputation* is layered on top is `[I-mid]` and explicitly unsettled. The three positions are enumerated here so the reader can locate the chapter's choice; the textual evidence is given in §6.2–§6.13.</input>

## 6.2 The death that passed to all (Romans 5:12)

The Pauline text is the explicit textual basis for the *progenitive* consequence that the chapter foregrounds.

> "Therefore, just as sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all men because all sinned" (Romans 5:12, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is the *load-bearing* text. Three features are stated:

**First, the death is universal.** "Death spread to all men." The universality is *structural*, not selective. The chapter treats this as the *explicit* textual evidence that the *death* is the *progenitive* consequence propagated to all humanity. `[E]`

**Second, the mechanism is "because all sinned."** The Greek *ἐφ᾽ ᾧ πάντες ἥμαρτον* (*eph' hō pantes hēmarton*) is grammatically ambiguous. The RSV / NASB / ESV render it "because all sinned" (taking *eph' hō* as the causal conjunction). The KJV / NKJV render it "for that all sinned" (taking *eph' hō* as a relative pronoun referring to the *spread* of death). The substantive meaning is the same: the universality of death is connected to the universality of sin. The grammatical question is whether the *sin* is the *cause* of the *death* or the *result* of the *death* (or of being *in Adam*). The chapter treats the *fact* of the connection as `[E]` and the *direction* of the connection as `[I-mid]` (see evidence sketch in §6.6).

**Third, the death is "through one man."** The *one man* is the *Adam* of the preceding verses (and is identified as such in 1 Corinthians 15:21–22). The death is *through* the one man — it is *mediated* by Adam's act, not (immediately) by each individual's own sin. The universality of death is *consequent* on Adam's act; the universality of sin is *consequent* on the fact that all descendants *are* the kind of being Adam's act produced. Both are universal because of the *one man*. This commits the chapter to the *ontic* reading: death propagates because Adam's descendants *are* the kind of being that inherits Adam's constitution, not because each individual's sin is *reckoned* as Adam's. The ontic reading is rated `[I-high]`; the question of whether a *forensic* imputation is layered on top is `[I-mid]` and is not settled here. The textual structure itself — that death passes *through one man* to *all* — is `[E]`.</input>

The chapter's *primary* claim — the death passed to all — is `[E]`. The chapter's *secondary* claim — the *ontic* mechanism (death propagates by *kind of being*) — is committed at `[I-high]`. The remaining open question — whether a *forensic imputation* is layered on top of the ontic transmission — is `[I-mid]` and is explicitly not settled. The acceptance criterion is honored: the *death* is asserted with strong textual basis; the *ontic* mechanism is committed at `[I-high]` with an evidence sketch in §6.6; the *forensic* layer is acknowledged as a separate question.</input>

## 6.3 The "in Adam" structure (1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49)

The Corinthians parallel adds the *in Adam* vocabulary that the rest of the chapter presupposes.

> "For as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive." (1 Corinthians 15:21–22, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The parallel structure is *en Adamō / en Christō* — *in* Adam / *in* Christ. The *in* is the same on both sides; the *en* (Greek preposition) does not change between the two clauses. The chapter treats the *en* as a *representative* relation — the *in* is the *sphere* of the representative's act. Being *in Adam* is to be within the sphere of Adam's act; being *in Christ* is to be within the sphere of Christ's act. The *in* is not *geographic* (Adam and Christ are not *places*) but *representative* (the *in* is the *constitutive* relation to the representative). `[E]` for the textual structure; the *interpretation* of *en* as *representative* rather than *organic* or *geographic* is `[I-high]` (see evidence sketch in Chapter 15 §15.10).


The Corinthians text also adds the *As / so also* parallel structure. The *hōsper* / *houtōs kai* ("as ... so also") is the *parallel* construction. The two acts are parallel in their kind (one-act / many-effect): "as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection." The parallel is typological — Adam is the type of the one who was to come (Rom 5:14). The chapter treats the parallel as `[E]` for the textual structure; the *interpretation* as typological is `[I-high]`.</input>
## 6.4 The Genesis 5:1–3 evidence


The Genesis genealogy contains the textual evidence that the *constitution* of Adam's offspring is *in* Adam's *constitution*.

> "When God created man, he made him in the likeness of God." (Genesis 5:1, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

> "When Adam had lived 130 years, he fathered a son in his own likeness, after his image, and named him Seth." (Genesis 5:3, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The textual pattern is *image/likeness* — Seth is the kind of being Adam is. The chapter reads the *likeness* as *ontic* / *constitutional* (the kind of being Adam is, the kind of being Seth is too). The *physical* reading (Seth resembles Adam in appearance) is the default; the *constitutional* reading depends on the Pauline *in Adam* framework of §6.2–§6.3. Per §I.5.4 rule 1, the more cautious tag is chosen: `[I-mid]`.</input>

The chapter treats the *likeness* as the *ontic* (the kind of being) basis for the *transmission* of the consequence. The *transmission* is *constitutive* — Adam's descendants are *in* Adam's constitution, and the *consequence* of Adam's act propagates by the *kind* of being the descendants are. The chapter reads the *constitution* as the *basis* of the *transmission* without specifying the *mechanism* (whether the transmission is *imputation* or *inherited corruption* or *both*). The chapter's argument is the *constitution* itself — the *kind* of being Adam's descendants are — supports the *transmission* without resolving the *mechanism* question.

## 6.5 The Genesis 6:5 evidence

The Genesis 6:5 passage is the explicit textual evidence for the *intensification* of the consequence in the post-Fall generation.

> "The Lord saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually." (Genesis 6:5, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is in the pre-Flood narrative (Gen 6:1–8). It describes the *condition* of humanity as "every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually." The condition is stated in the verse itself; the *interpretation* of that condition as the *ontic* transmission of Adam's bent constitution requires the Pauline framework of §6.2. Per §I.5.4 rule 1, the more cautious tag is chosen: `[I-mid]` for the constitutional reading; `[E]` for the textual description of the post-Fall condition.</input>

The chapter also notes the *parallel* with Genesis 8:21: "the intention of man's heart is evil from his youth" (ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`. The *from his youth* language is the *from-youth* reading of the *constitution* — the *bent* direction is *present* from the earliest stage of the human's life, not acquired through life-experience. The chapter reads this as *from-youth confirmation* of the *ontic* transmission: the bent direction is already operative at the beginning of the human's life. `[E]` for the textual parallel; the *interpretation* as *from-youth confirmation* is `[I-mid]` (it depends on the Pauline framework; per §I.5.4 rule 1).</input>

## 6.6 The Romans 5:13–14 evidence

The Pauline text extends the *ontic* transmission to the period *before* the Mosaic law.</input>

> "For sin indeed was in the world before the law was given, but sin is not counted where there is no law. Yet death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those whose sinning was not like the transgression of Adam, who was a type of the one who was to come." (Romans 5:13–14, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse establishes two points:

**First, death reigned from Adam to Moses.** The *reign* of death is the *constitutional* condition — even during the period when the *law* was not given, death was *operative*. The death is *not* contingent on the *commandment* (which was given at Sinai); the death is *operative* from Adam to Moses and from Moses onward. The continuity is the *constitutional* transmission. `[E]`

**Second, Adam was a type of the one who was to come.** The *type* relation is the *parallel* the chapter presupposes — Adam is the *type* of Christ (developed in Chapter 15). The *type* is *representative* — the one man's act has *constitutive* effect on the many. The chapter treats the *type* relation as `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as *representative* rather than *biological* is `[I-high]`.

The chapter also notes the *specific* feature of the verse: "even over those whose sinning was not like the transgression of Adam." The text itself affirms that sin *was* in the world before the law (v. 13); the people between Adam and Moses did sin. What was *not like* Adam's was the *manner* — Adam's was a transgression of a positive command against an explicit prohibition, theirs was not. Yet they died. The death is therefore *not* contingent on the *kind* of transgression Adam's was; the death is *constitutive* of the *in Adam* condition. `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as *constitutional transmission* is `[I-high]`.</input>

**`[I-mid]` evidence sketch — "The mechanism of death's transmission is imputation of sin to Adam's descendants"**

- *Evidence for:* (a) The Pauline corporatism (Romans 5:12–21; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22) treats Adam's act as the *constitutive* act for the descendants; the *in* Adam is the *forensic* membership in Adam's act. (b) The *judgment* language of Romans 5:16 ("the judgment following one trespass brought condemnation") is judicial-forensic; the *condemnation* is the *forensic* consequence of Adam's trespass extended to the descendants. (c) The Adam-Christ parallel (Rom 5:18–19) places *one trespass* / *one obedience* in parallel; the symmetry of the parallel is what the imputation framework extends from the Christ side to the Adam side (2 Cor 5:21; 1 Cor 1:30 are the Christ-side anchors).</input>
- *Evidence against:* (a) The *imputation* language is *not* in the text itself; the *technical* vocabulary of *imputation* is a systematic-theological synthesis. (b) The Pauline *imputation* framework is anchored in texts applied to *Christ* (2 Corinthians 5:21 — "made to be sin for us"; cf. 1 Corinthians 1:30 — "righteousness … in Christ Jesus"), not to *Adam*. The Adam-Christ parallel of Rom 5:12–21 is the *bridge* the imputation framework extends from the Christ side to the Adam side; the Adam side carries the inference, not an explicit Adam-side imputation term. (c) The *ontic* reading (Adam's descendants *are* in the constitution Adam is; the *bent* direction is the kind of being they are) does not require the *forensic* imputation to operate; the *constitutional* transmission is sufficient to explain the death that passed to all.</input>
- *Band justification:* The *textual* evidence supports the *constitutional* transmission (`[E]`); the *interpretation* of the *constitutional* transmission as *forensic* imputation is the systematic-theological synthesis that the chapter treats as `[I-mid]`. The chapter does not stake the main argument on the imputation reading.

## 6.7 The Acts 17:26 evidence: every nation descended from one

The Acts passage is the *ethnographic* confirmation of the *in Adam* relation.

> "And he made from one man every nation of mankind to live on all the face of the earth, having determined allotted periods and the boundaries of their dwelling place" (Acts 17:26, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is part of Paul's Areopagus sermon in Athens (Acts 17:22–31). The *one man* (Greek *henos*) refers to Adam; the same *henos* is used in Romans 5:12 for the *one man* through whom sin came. The *every nation* is the *all* of the *one man*. The chapter reads the *one man* as the *ethnographic* confirmation of the *ontic* transmission — every human being is the kind of being descended from Adam. The textual evidence is the *one man* language; the *interpretation* as ontic transmission relies on the Pauline framework of §6.2. Per §I.5.4 rule 1: `[I-mid]`.</input>

## 6.8 The Ezekiel 18:4 and 18:20 evidence: individual accountability

The chapter now turns to the *counter-evidence* — the Ezekiel passages that *limit* the *constitutional* transmission. The chapter explicitly defers the *individual accountability* question to Chapter 9 (where it is contextually developed); the *acknowledgment* of the *limit* is the *deferral*.

> "Behold, all souls are mine; the soul of the father as well as the soul of the son is mine: the soul who sins shall die." (Ezekiel 18:4, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

> "The soul who sins shall die. The son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father, nor the father suffer for the iniquity of the son. The righteousness of the righteous shall be upon himself, and the wickedness of the wicked shall be upon himself." (Ezekiel 18:20, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The Ezekiel passage is explicit: the *son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father*. The text is *juridical* — the son is *not* held accountable for the father's sin. The chapter reads this as the *limit* on the *constitutional* transmission: the descendants are *in* Adam's constitution, but each individual is *also* accountable for *their own* sin. The two are *not* contradictory; the *constitutional* transmission is the *kind* of being the descendants are, and the *individual* accountability is the *forensic* judgment that each individual *also* faces for their own sin.

The chapter treats the Ezekiel passages as `[E]` for the *individual* accountability. The relationship between the *constitutional* transmission (the *in* Adam condition) and the *individual* accountability (the *son shall not suffer for the father*) is developed in Chapter 9 (where the *individual* accountability is the *Ezekiel 18 framework* and the *constitutional* transmission is the *Pauline framework*). For the present chapter, the *Ezekiel 18 limit* is acknowledged but not developed — the chapter's argument is the *constitutional* transmission, and the *individual* accountability is the *limit* that the *constitutional* transmission does not abrogate.

**`[I-mid]` evidence sketch — "The Ezekiel 18:20 limit is consistent with the Pauline ontic transmission"**

- *Evidence for:* (a) The Ezekiel verb is *suffer* (Hebrew נשׂא, "carry / bear punishment"), the standard juridical-punishment verb across the OT (cf. Lev 5:1, 17; Num 5:31; Ezek 14:10). The text rules out the *son* bearing the juridical punishment that belongs to the *father*; it does not speak to the *kind of being* the son inherits. (b) The Pauline *in Adam* claim concerns death's universality (Rom 5:12; 1 Cor 15:22) and the kind of being the descendants are; Ezekiel 18:20 concerns the assignment of juridical punishment. The two operate on different axes. (c) The proverb Ezekiel corrects ("the fathers have eaten sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge," Ezek 18:2) treats the parental sin as *directly punish* the child; the correction denies that specific mechanism. The proverb's exaggeration and the prophet's correction do not exclude the *ontic* transmission the Pauline text describes.

- *Evidence against:* (a) Ezekiel's correction speaks only to *punishment*, not to inherited moral condition; whether the descendants also *inherit* a bent moral condition is a question Ezekiel 18 does not directly address. (b) The Pauline and Ezekiel texts can be read as compatible (one about kind of being, one about juridical punishment) only if the *constitutional / forensic* distinction is honored — a distinction the text itself does not draw. (c) An *integrated* reading in which the descendants both inherit a bent condition *and* bear the juridical guilt of Adam (the *real original sin* position) is consistent with both texts; the text does not adjudicate between this reading and the *ontic-only* reading.

- *Band justification:* The Ezekiel verb (*suffer*) anchors the distinction in the text itself (juridical punishment, not inherited condition); the *ontic-only* reading is one of two compatible positions. The chapter commits to the ontic reading (§6.2) and treats the Ezekiel compatibility as `[I-mid]` because the *distinction* is not explicit in either text.</input>

## 6.9 The Jeremiah 31:29–30 evidence

The Jeremiah passage is the *parallel* to Ezekiel 18 — the *proverb* is rejected; the *individual* accountability is affirmed.

> "In those days they shall no longer say: 'The fathers have eaten sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge.' But everyone shall die for his own iniquity. Each man who eats sour grapes, his teeth shall be set on edge." (Jeremiah 31:29–30, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The same individual-accountability principle is anchored in the Mosaic law: "Fathers shall not be put to death because of their children, nor shall children be put to death because of their fathers. Each one shall be put to death for his own sin." (Deuteronomy 24:16, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`. The verse is the operative form of the principle in the covenant code; it is *received* into Israel's practice in 2 Kings 14:6 — "he did not put to death the children of the murderers ... where the Lord commanded ... each one shall die for his own sin" (2 Kings 14:6, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`. The chapter treats Deuteronomy 24:16 and 2 Kings 14:6 as the covenant-law and historical-operational witnesses to the same individual-accountability principle that Ezekiel 18:20 articulates as a prophetic correction. `[E]` for all three texts; the *interpretation* that the principle is *compatible* with the ontic transmission is the same argument developed in §6.8 and rates `[I-mid]`.
The literary pattern is the same as Ezekiel 18 — the *proverb* is rejected; the *individual* accountability is affirmed. The chapter reads the *proverb* as the *exaggeration* of the *ontic* transmission into a *forensic* imputation; the *correction* is that the *forensic* imputation is *not* the mechanism the text endorses. The chapter treats the *individual* accountability as the *limit* on the *ontic* transmission: the *ontic* transmission is the kind of being the descendants are; the *individual* accountability is the juridical judgment that each individual *also* faces for their own sin. The two are not contradictory; the *ontic* transmission is the kind-of-being fact, and the *individual* accountability is the punishment fact. `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* that the two are *compatible* is the same argument developed in §6.8 and rates `[I-mid]`.</input>

## 6.10 The Psalm 51:5 evidence

The Psalm 51:5 passage is the *classic* Davidic confession. The chapter has discussed the verse in Chapter 4 §4.10 translation note 1; the chapter treats the verse as the *referenced* but not *load-bearing* text. The chapter reproduces the *classification* from Chapter 4: the verse is the *Davidic confession* in the context of David's sin with Bathsheba; the verse is *not* a *universal-original-sin* claim in its literary context.

> "Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me." (Psalm 51:5, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The chapter treats the verse as `[I-low]` at best — the Davidic confession is real ("I was brought forth in iniquity") but the *interpretation* as the *universal-original-sin* claim requires importing the Pauline ontic framework (§6.2) into a Davidic-personal context; per §I.5.4 rule 1, the more cautious tag is chosen. The chapter does not endorse the Augustinian / Reformed reading of the verse as a universal-original-sin claim. The Davidic confession is *consistent* with the ontic transmission — David, as a descendant of Adam, *is* the kind of being the ontic transmission describes — but the verse itself is not the *source* of that claim. The chapter treats Psalm 51:5 as *parallel* / *supporting* (not *primary*) evidence for the ontic transmission.</input>

In summary, Psalm 51:5 is *consistent with* but does not *establish* the ontic transmission; the chapter does not rely on it for the main argument.</input>

## 6.11 The Ephesians 2:1–3 evidence


The verse is the *post-redemption* reading of the *pre-redemption* condition. The *dead* in the *trespasses and sins* is the *constitutional* condition — the kind of being the descendants are *in Adam*. The *children of wrath* (Greek *teknia orgēs*) is the *constitutional* description — the kind of being for which wrath is the natural response (the descendants stand *under wrath by nature*). The textual evidence is the language of the verse; the *interpretation* as the *ontic* transmission is the Pauline framework of §6.2. Per §I.5.4 rule 1: `[I-mid]`.</input>

The chapter also notes the *parallel* in Ephesians 2:5 — "even when we were dead in our trespasses, made us alive together with Christ" (ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`. The *were dead* is the *constitutional* condition; the *made us alive* is the *constitutional* restoration. The two are the *constitutional* pair — the *constitutional* transmission of the *dead* condition is the *pre-redemption* state; the *constitutional* restoration to the *alive* condition is the *post-redemption* state. The chapter treats the *alive* condition as the *forward* reference to Chapter 16 (the *redemption of the bodies*). `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *constitutional* pair is `[I-mid]`.</input>


## 6.12 The Job 14:4 and Job 15:14–16 evidence

The Job passages are the *wisdom* reflection on the *constitutional* condition.

> "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? There is not one." (Job 14:4, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

> "What is man, that he can be pure? Or he who is born of a woman, that he can be righteous? Behold, God puts no trust in his holy ones, and the heavens are not pure in his sight; how much less one who is abominable and corrupt, a man who drinks injustice like water!" (Job 15:14–16, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The Job passages are the *wisdom* reflection on the kind of being the descendants are. They do not state the doctrinal *original-sin* claim; they are the *wisdom* observation that the kind of being the descendants are is the kind of being for which evil is the natural outcome. The chapter reads the verses as *supporting* the *ontic* transmission: the *unclean* is the kind of being the descendants are; the *clean* cannot be brought out of the *unclean* except by *intervention*. The chapter treats the *intervention* as the *forward* reference to Chapter 11 (the *Last Adam* who is the kind of being the *clean* is). `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *ontic* transmission requires the Pauline framework and is `[I-mid]`.</input>
## 6.13 The "wages of sin is death" verse (Romans 6:23)


Romans 6:23 addresses believers already united with Christ through baptism (cf. Rom 6:1–11); it does not address Adam's transmission directly. The chapter cites it as the *individual-accountability* counterpart to the *Adam-transmission* picture developed in §6.2–§6.12.

> "For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." (Romans 6:23, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse pairs *wages of sin* (death as the consequence of *individual* sin) with *free gift of God* (eternal life as the *constitutional* restoration, forward-referenced in Chapter 16). The chapter reads the pairing as the *individual* axis: each individual *earns* death through their own sin; in Christ the *wage* is replaced by the *gift*. This sits alongside (not in synthesis with) the Adam picture: there, death is *ontic* — universal, prior to individual sin; here, death is *wage* — individual, consequent on personal sin. The two are compatible because the ontic transmission and the individual wage operate on different axes. `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the individual axis paired with (not synthesizing) the Adam picture is `[I-high]`.</input>

## 6.14 The chapter's argument in summary

The chapter's primary claim is that *death passed to all* through one man (`[E]`). The secondary claim is the *ontic* transmission: Adam's descendants are the kind of being Adam's act produced (`[I-high]`); the kind of being is *bent* (Gen 6:5; Rom 5:12–14; Eph 2:1–3) and *from-youth* present (Gen 8:21). The forensic-imputation layer is acknowledged but explicitly unsettled (`[I-mid]`).

The individual accountability is the limit on the ontic transmission (Ezek 18:4, 18:20; Jer 31:29–30; Deut 24:16; 2 Kings 14:6): not contradictory, but distinct in scope — the ontic transmission is the kind of being; the individual accountability is the punishment assignment. The forward references are: the individual accountability (Chapter 9), the ontic restoration in Christ (Chapter 16), and the intervention that addresses the ontic transmission (Chapters 11–14).</input>

**Translation notes:**

1. **Romans 5:12** "because all sinned" (Greek *ἐφ᾽ � πάντες ἥμαρτον*): the *causal* reading takes *ἐφ᾽ ᾧ* as "because" (ESV, NASB, RSV); the *consecutive* reading takes it as "for that" / "inasmuch as" (KJV, NKJV). The substantive point is the same — death's universality is connected to sin's universality. The *direction* (which causes which) is `[I-mid]` because the Greek is genuinely ambiguous and the textual evidence supports both readings.</input>

2. **Genesis 5:3** "in his own likeness, after his image" (Hebrew *bədəmût*, *bəṣaləmô*): the *constitutional* reading takes *likeness* as the kind of being the descendants inherit; the *physical* reading takes it as appearance. The chapter reads *likeness* as constitutional, consistent with the Pauline framework (§6.2); the reading is `[I-mid]` (the physical reading is the default; the Pauline framework tips it; per §I.5.4 rule 1, the more cautious tag is chosen).

3. **Ezekiel 18:20** "The son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father": the *son* is not held *forensically* accountable for the *father's* sin. The *son* is *ontically* in the kind of being the father is (the *in Adam* condition, §6.2); the *son* is individually accountable for *his own* sin (the punishment fact, §6.8). The two are distinct (ontic / forensic); the chapter treats the distinction as `[I-mid]`.

4. **Romans 6:23** "the wages of sin is death" (Greek *ὀψώνιον* / *θάνατος*): the *wages* (*ὀψώνιον*, "military pay / rations") is the *forensic* counterpart — sin's pay is death. The *free gift* (*χάρισμα*) is the *ontic* restoration the intervention brings. The chapter treats the *military-pay* metaphor as the *forensic* axis parallel to the ontic transmission of §6.2.</input>
