## 6.1 The question

Chapter 5 inventoried what Adam immediately lost. This chapter asks what *passed* from Adam to his descendants. The primary claim is that *death passed to all* — this is explicit (`[E]`). The mechanism is ontic: Adam's descendants are the kind of being his act produced (`[I-high]`). Whether a forensic imputation of guilt is layered on top is left open at `[I-mid]`.

The chapter distinguishes three possible positions for the reader:
1. Imputation of sin (forensic).
2. Inherited corruption (ontic).
3. Both.

It commits to the ontic reading at `[I-high]` and treats the forensic layer as unsettled. Ezekiel 18:20 ("the son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father") is acknowledged as a limit on direct imputation and deferred to Chapter 9.

## 6.2 Death passed to all (Romans 5:12)

> "Therefore, just as sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all men because all sinned." (Romans 5:12, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

Death is universal and mediated through one man. The Greek *eph' hō pantes hēmarton* is ambiguous, but the fact of the connection between Adam's act and universal death is explicit.

**Tagging:** `[E]` for universality through one man. `[I-mid]` for the precise direction of causation.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-mid]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) "Death spread to all men" is stated directly. (b) The parallel with 1 Cor 15:22 ("in Adam all die") reinforces universality.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The grammar of *eph' hō* allows both causal and consecutive readings. (b) Some interpretations emphasize individual sin as the immediate cause.
- *Band justification:* Universality through one man is explicit; the exact mechanism is an inference from context. `[I-mid]` is honest.

## 6.3 The "in Adam" structure (1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49)

> "For as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive." (1 Corinthians 15:21–22, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

> "The first man Adam became a living being; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit... The first man was from the earth, a man of dust; the second man is from heaven." (1 Corinthians 15:45, 47, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The parallel is *in Adam / in Christ*. The preposition *en* marks representative relation. Adam is "the first man"; Christ is "the last Adam" and "the second man." The titles use the same category for both.

**Tagging:** `[E]` for the parallel structure and titles. `[I-high]` for the representative reading of *en*.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-high]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) The same *en* is used on both sides. (b) The titles *first man* and *second man* / *last Adam* name both by humanity. (c) Romans 5:14 calls Adam "a type of the one who was to come."
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) "From heaven" and "life-giving spirit" qualify Christ's side. (b) Biological descent language is also present.
- *Band justification:* The representative reading best explains the symmetry and is reinforced by Romans 5. `[I-high]` is honest.

## 6.4 Genesis 5:1–3 — constitutional likeness

> "When God created man, he made him in the likeness of God. When Adam had lived 130 years, he fathered a son in his own likeness, after his image, and named him Seth." (Genesis 5:1, 3, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The pattern is *image/likeness*. Seth is "in his own likeness, after his image." This supports the ontic reading: descendants are the kind of being Adam is. The physical reading is default; the constitutional reading depends on the Pauline framework.

**Tagging:** `[E]` for the likeness language. `[I-mid]` for the constitutional reading (physical sense is primary; Pauline parallel tips it toward ontic).

**Evidence sketch (`[I-mid]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) The same *likeness/image* language used for Adam's creation is used for Seth. (b) The Pauline "in Adam" framework supplies the constitutional sense.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The immediate sense is physical resemblance. (b) The verse does not explicitly say "constitutional transmission."
- *Band justification:* Likeness is explicit; the constitutional interpretation is a reasonable inference from the larger Pauline context. `[I-mid]` is honest.

## 6.5 Genesis 6:5 and 8:21 — the persistent condition

> "The Lord saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually." (Genesis 6:5, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

> "The intention of man's heart is evil from his youth." (Genesis 8:21, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

These verses describe a condition present "continually" and "from his youth." They support the ontic reading: the bent direction is part of the kind of being Adam's descendants are. They do not by themselves prove the mechanism of transmission.

**Tagging:** `[E]` for the textual description. `[I-mid]` for the ontic-transmission reading (the verses describe the condition; the link to Adam's act is inferential).

**Evidence sketch (`[I-mid]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) "Every intention... only evil continually" and "from his youth" show the condition is pervasive and early. (b) Placement after the flood shows it was not removed by judgment.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The verses do not name Adam or explain causation. (b) They could be read as moral observation rather than ontological claim.
- *Band justification:* The condition is explicit; the ontic link to Adam is a reasonable inference from the larger canonical context. `[I-mid]` is honest.

## 6.6 Romans 5:13–14 and the limit of Ezekiel 18:20

Romans 5:13–14 states that death reigned from Adam to Moses even over those whose sinning was not like Adam's transgression. This reinforces that death is not contingent on individual transgression of positive law. Ezekiel 18:20 ("the son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father") is acknowledged as a limit on direct imputation of guilt. The two are compatible if constitutional transmission (ontic condition) and individual accountability (forensic judgment) operate on different axes. The chapter defers full engagement of Ezekiel 18:20 to Chapter 9.

**Tagging:** `[E]` for both texts. `[I-mid]` for the compatibility of the two axes (the distinction is inferential).

**Evidence sketch (`[I-mid]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) Romans 5:14 explicitly says death reigned even when sin was not like Adam's. (b) Ezekiel 18:20 limits juridical punishment to the individual. (c) The two statements address different questions (universal condition vs. individual punishment).
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) Neither text explicitly draws the ontic/forensic distinction. (b) An integrated imputation reading is also possible.
- *Band justification:* The texts are compatible on different axes; the distinction is a reasonable synthesis but not explicit. `[I-mid]` is honest.

## 6.7 Ephesians 2:1–3 — the constitutional condition after redemption

> "And you were dead in the trespasses and sins in which you once walked, following the course of this world, following the prince of the power of the air, the spirit that is now at work in the sons of disobedience — among whom we all once lived in the passions of our flesh, carrying out the desires of the flesh and the mind, and were by nature children of wrath, like the rest of mankind." (Ephesians 2:1–3, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The *dead in trespasses and sins* and *by nature children of wrath* language describes the constitutional condition inherited from Adam. The *we all once lived* includes the writer and his readers — the condition is universal. The chapter reads this as the post-redemption description of the ontic transmission: the kind of being the descendants are *in Adam* is the kind of being that stands under wrath by nature.

**Tagging:** `[E]` for the textual description. `[I-high]` for the ontic-transmission reading (the language of "by nature" and "we all once lived" strongly supports the constitutional condition).

**Evidence sketch (`[I-high]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) "By nature children of wrath" is a constitutional description. (b) "We all once lived" includes the redeemed — the condition is universal. (c) The parallel with Rom 5:12 ("death spread to all") reinforces the ontic reading.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The verse is addressed to believers; the "once" language could be read as past behavior rather than inherited condition. (b) "Children of wrath" could be read as moral state rather than constitutional.
- *Band justification:* The "by nature" and "we all once lived" language strongly supports the constitutional reading when read with Romans 5. `[I-high]` is honest.

## 6.8 Psalm 51:5 — the Davidic confession (handle with care)

> "Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me." (Psalm 51:5, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

This is David's personal confession in the context of his sin with Bathsheba. The verse is the *classic* Davidic confession. It is *not* a universal-original-sin claim in its literary context. The chapter treats it as `[I-low]` at best — the Davidic confession is real ("I was brought forth in iniquity") but the interpretation as a universal claim requires importing the Pauline ontic framework. The verse is consistent with the ontic transmission (David, as a descendant of Adam, is the kind of being the ontic transmission describes) but is not the source of that claim. It is treated as parallel/supporting evidence rather than primary.

**Tagging:** `[E]` for the verse. `[I-low]` for the universal-original-sin reading (the literary context is Davidic confession; the universal reading is an inference).

**Evidence sketch (`[I-low]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) The verse uses strong language of being brought forth in iniquity. (b) It aligns with the ontic transmission described in Romans 5.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The immediate context is David's personal confession after his own sin, not a universal statement. (b) The verse does not mention Adam or transmission to all humanity.
- *Band justification:* The confession is explicit; the universal reading is a possible inference but not the immediate context. `[I-low]` is honest. The verse is handled with care per the outline (E6.3).

## 6.9 Job 14:4 and Job 15:14–16 — the wisdom reflection

> "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? There is not one." (Job 14:4, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

> "What is man, that he can be pure? Or he who is born of a woman, that he can be righteous? Behold, God puts no trust in his holy ones, and the heavens are not pure in his sight; how much less one who is abominable and corrupt, a man who drinks injustice like water!" (Job 15:14–16, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

These are wisdom reflections on the human condition. They do not state a doctrinal original-sin claim; they are observations that the kind of being humans are is the kind of being for which evil is the natural outcome. The chapter reads them as supporting the ontic transmission: the unclean cannot produce the clean except by intervention. The intervention is the forward reference to Chapter 11 (the last Adam who is the kind of being the clean is).

**Tagging:** `[E]` for the textual description. `[I-mid]` for the ontic-transmission reading (the verses describe the condition; the link to Adam's act is inferential).

**Evidence sketch (`[I-mid]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean?" directly supports the idea that the human condition is unclean. (b) "Born of a woman" links the condition to birth, consistent with descent from Adam.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The verses are wisdom poetry, not systematic theology. (b) They do not name Adam or explain the mechanism of transmission.
- *Band justification:* The condition is explicit; the ontic link to Adam is a reasonable inference from the larger canonical context. `[I-mid]` is honest.

## 6.10 Acts 17:26 — every nation descended from one

> "And he made from one man every nation of mankind to live on all the face of the earth, having determined allotted periods and the boundaries of their dwelling place." (Acts 17:26, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

Paul's Areopagus sermon states that every nation is made from one man (Adam). The *one man* language is the same as in Romans 5:12. This is the ethnographic confirmation of the *in Adam* relation: every human being is the kind of being descended from Adam. The textual evidence is the *one man* language; the interpretation as ontic transmission relies on the Pauline framework.

**Tagging:** `[E]` for the *one man* language. `[I-mid]` for the ontic-transmission reading.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-mid]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) The *one man* is the same phrase used in Romans 5:12 for Adam. (b) The verse grounds the unity of humanity in descent from one man.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The immediate purpose is to affirm God's sovereignty over nations, not to teach original sin. (b) The verse does not specify the mechanism of transmission.
- *Band justification:* The *one man* language is explicit; the ontic reading is a reasonable inference from the Pauline parallel. `[I-mid]` is honest.

## 6.11 Romans 6:23 — the individual accountability counterpart

> "For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." (Romans 6:23, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

This verse addresses believers already united with Christ. It pairs *wages of sin* (death as the consequence of individual sin) with *free gift of God* (eternal life as the constitutional restoration). It sits alongside the Adam picture: there, death is ontic — universal, prior to individual sin; here, death is wage — individual, consequent on personal sin. The two are compatible because the ontic transmission and the individual wage operate on different axes.

**Tagging:** `[E]` for the textual pairing. `[I-mid]` for the individual-axis reading paired with (not synthesizing) the Adam picture.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-mid]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) "Wages of sin is death" is the individual forensic counterpart. (b) The *free gift* is the restoration parallel to Chapter 16.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The verse is addressed to believers; it does not directly discuss Adam's transmission.
- *Band justification:* The pairing is explicit; the individual-axis reading is a reasonable inference from the context. `[I-mid]` is honest.

## 6.12 Jeremiah 31:29–30 — the parallel to Ezekiel 18

> "In those days they shall no longer say: 'The fathers have eaten sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge.' But everyone shall die for his own iniquity. Each man who eats sour grapes, his teeth shall be set on edge." (Jeremiah 31:29–30, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The same individual-accountability principle is anchored in the Mosaic law (Deut 24:16) and received into Israel's practice (2 Kgs 14:6). The literary pattern is the same as Ezekiel 18 — the proverb is rejected; individual accountability is affirmed. The chapter reads the proverb as the exaggeration of the ontic transmission into a forensic imputation; the correction is that the forensic imputation is not the mechanism the text endorses. The two (ontic transmission and individual accountability) are not contradictory; they operate on different axes.

**Tagging:** `[E]` for the text. `[I-mid]` for the compatibility reading (the axes distinction is inferential).

**Evidence sketch (`[I-mid]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) The proverb is explicitly rejected. (b) "Everyone shall die for his own iniquity" limits punishment to the individual.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The text does not explicitly address ontic transmission. (b) An integrated imputation reading is possible.
- *Band justification:* The individual accountability is explicit; the compatibility with ontic transmission is a reasonable synthesis. `[I-mid]` is honest.

## 6.13 Synthesis

The primary claim is that *death passed to all* through one man (`[E]`). The secondary claim is ontic transmission: Adam's descendants are the kind of being his act produced (`[I-high]`). Forensic imputation of guilt is left open at `[I-mid]`. Ezekiel 18:20 and Jeremiah 31:29–30 are the limits on direct imputation and are engaged fairly as complementary to the ontic condition. The chapter distinguishes what the text explicitly says from what it implies, avoiding over-claim on the mechanism of guilt.
