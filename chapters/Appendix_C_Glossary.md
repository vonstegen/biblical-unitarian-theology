This appendix defines the project's technical terms with biblical references and tags. The definitions are drawn from the text itself; no systematic-theological synthesis is imposed. The glossary is non-blocking but required for the manuscript to be presented as finished.

## C.1 Debt

The term "debt" is the project's description of the consequence of Adam's disobedience. The text does not use the commercial metaphor "debt" for Adam's act; the project uses it as a shorthand for the *obligation* Adam incurred before God by his disobedience and the *penalty* God established as the consequence (Genesis 2:16–17 — "you shall surely die"). The obligation is the failure to render the obedience the covenant required; the penalty is death. The term is `[I-mid]` for the commercial metaphor (the text uses *hamartia* for sin and *thanatos* for death; the commercial metaphor is a synthesis from the *lytron* and *apolytrosis* language of redemption in the New Testament — Mark 10:45; Romans 3:24; 1 Timothy 2:6). The project uses "debt" as a convenient shorthand for the obligation and the penalty; the reader should evaluate the metaphor against the text.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-mid]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) The *lytron* ("ransom") and *apolytrosis* ("redemption") language in the New Testament implies a price paid for release (Mark 10:45; Romans 3:24; 1 Timothy 2:6). (b) The parallel with the commercial language of forgiveness in the parable of the unforgiving servant (Matthew 18:21–35) supports the metaphor.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The text does not use the commercial term "debt" for Adam's act. (b) The primary language is *hamartia* (sin) and *thanatos* (death). (c) The commercial metaphor can obscure the personal and relational character of the covenant breach.
- *Band justification:* The commercial metaphor is a reasonable synthesis from the *lytron* and *apolytrosis* language; the text itself uses *hamartia* and *thanatos*. The metaphor is useful but not explicit. `[I-mid]` is honest.

## C.2 Corresponding Justice

The term "corresponding justice" is the project's description of the formal constraint that makes the debt-debtor framework coherent. The text does not use the phrase; the project uses it as a shorthand for the principle that the penalty must be paid by a representative of the same order as the debtor (man for man). The principle is drawn from the Adam-Christ parallel (Romans 5:18–19 — "as by the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners, so by the one man's obedience the many will be made righteous") and the explicit humanity of the mediator (1 Timothy 2:5 — "the man Christ Jesus"). The term is `[I-high]` for the principle (the parallel is explicit; the "corresponding" language is the chapter's synthesis). The project uses "corresponding justice" as the formal constraint that the substitute must be of the same order as the debtor; the reader should evaluate the principle against the text.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-high]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) Romans 5:18–19 — the parallel is one man to one man, disobedience to obedience, condemnation to justification. (b) 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45, 47 — "by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection"; "the last Adam," "the second man." (c) 1 Timothy 2:5 — "the man Christ Jesus" as the mediator.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The parallel could be read as symbolic rather than structural. (b) The "man" language could be read as the human nature of a divine person rather than a human representative of the same order as Adam.
- *Band justification:* The parallel is explicit across Romans 5 and 1 Corinthians 15; the "corresponding" language is the natural reading of the one-man / one-man structure. The principle is `[I-high]`: strong implication from immediate context plus clear parallels; would be `[E]` if the text stated "the substitute must be of the same order as the debtor."

## C.3 Federal Headship

The term "federal headship" is the project's description of the scriptural mechanism by which one man's obedience suffices for the many. The text does not use the term; the project uses it as a shorthand for the representative structure of the Adam-Christ parallel (Romans 5:18–19 — "by the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners, so by the one man's obedience the many will be made righteous"; 1 Corinthians 15:22 — "as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive"). The term is `[I]` for the vocabulary (Paul does not use the term; the Bible's own vocabulary is the *one-for-many* structure); the structural observations are `[E]` (the parallel and the universality are stated in the text). The project uses "federal headship" for clarity but the substance is the structural observations, not the vocabulary. The reader should evaluate the structure against the text.

**Evidence sketch (`[I]` for the vocabulary; `[E]` for the structural observations):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) Romans 5:18–19 — the one-man / many structure is explicit. (b) 1 Corinthians 15:22 — "in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive." (c) The universality on both sides is structural, not selective.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The term "federal headship" is not in the text. (b) The "in Adam" / "in Christ" relation could be read as biological descent or organic union rather than representative headship.
- *Band justification:* The structural observations are explicit in the text; the vocabulary "federal headship" is a systematic-theological name for the observations. The vocabulary is `[I]`; the structural observations are `[E]`.

## C.4 Ontic Transmission

The term "ontic transmission" is the project's description of the mechanism by which the consequence of Adam's act propagates to his descendants. The text does not use the term; the project uses it as a shorthand for the kind of being Adam's descendants are (the bent condition described in Genesis 6:5 — "every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" — and Genesis 8:21 — "the intention of man's heart is evil from his youth"). The term is `[I-high]` for the mechanism (the condition is explicit; the link to Adam's act is a strong implication from the Pauline framework in Romans 5:12 and 1 Corinthians 15:22). The project uses "ontic transmission" to distinguish the inherited condition from the forensic imputation of guilt (left open at `[I-mid]` in Chapter 6). The reader should evaluate the mechanism against the text.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-high]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) Genesis 6:5 and 8:21 describe the condition as pervasive and "from his youth." (b) Romans 5:12 — "death spread to all men because all sinned." (c) 1 Corinthians 15:22 — "in Adam all die."
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The verses do not explicitly link the condition to Adam's act. (b) The condition could be read as moral observation rather than ontological transmission.
- *Band justification:* The condition is explicit; the link to Adam's act is a strong implication from the Pauline framework. `[I-high]` is honest.

## C.5 Forensic Judgment

The term "forensic judgment" is the project's description of how God holds individuals accountable for their own sin. The text does not use the term; the project uses it as a shorthand for the principle that the son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father (Ezekiel 18:20; Jeremiah 31:29–30; Deuteronomy 24:16; 2 Kings 14:6). The term is `[I-mid]` for the distinction from the ontic transmission (the text limits juridical punishment to the individual; the distinction between ontic condition and forensic judgment is inferential). The project uses "forensic judgment" to distinguish the personal accountability from the inherited condition (the ontic transmission). The reader should evaluate the principle against the text.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-mid]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) Ezekiel 18:20 — "the son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father." (b) Jeremiah 31:29–30 — the proverb is rejected; everyone shall die for his own iniquity. (c) Deuteronomy 24:16 and 2 Kings 14:6 — the principle is received into Israel's practice.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The text does not explicitly contrast "forensic" and "ontic." (b) An integrated reading in which the descendants both inherit a bent condition and bear the juridical guilt of Adam is possible.
- *Band justification:* The limit on punishment to the individual is explicit; the distinction from the ontic transmission is a reasonable synthesis but not explicit. `[I-mid]` is honest.

## C.6 Willing Obedience

The term "willing obedience" is the project's description of Christ's obedience as the willing submission of his will to the Father's will. The text does not use the term; the project uses it as a shorthand for the Gethsemane prayer ("not as I will, but as you will" — Matthew 26:39; Mark 14:36; Luke 22:42) and the Johannine statements ("not to do my own will but the will of him who sent me" — John 6:38; "I always do the things that are pleasing to him" — John 8:29). The term is `[E]` for the textual language (the "not my will" and "not my own will" are explicit); the *interpretation* as the standing form of the Son's relation to the Father is `[I-high]` (the Johannine parallels confirm the standing reading). The project uses "willing obedience" to distinguish the obedience from mechanical or coerced obedience. The reader should evaluate the language against the text.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-high]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) The Gethsemane prayer explicitly names the Son's will set against the cup and the submission to the Father's will. (b) John 6:38 places "not my own will but the will of him who sent me" as the baseline. (c) John 8:29's "always" makes the subordination uninterrupted.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The "not my will" could be read as a conventional phrase rather than a real counter-will. (b) The obedience could be read as the divine will operating through the human nature.
- *Band justification:* The "not my will" and "not my own will" language is explicit; the standing form is the natural reading of the Johannine parallels. `[I-high]` is honest.

## C.7 Last Adam

The term "last Adam" is the project's description of Christ as the corresponding human representative who answers the debt incurred by the first Adam. The text uses the term explicitly in 1 Corinthians 15:45 ("the last Adam became a life-giving spirit"). The term is `[E]` for the textual title; the *interpretation* as the corresponding human representative who renders the obedience the first Adam failed to render is `[I-high]` (the parallel with the first Adam in Romans 5:12–21 and 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49 is explicit). The project uses "last Adam" as the biblical title for the obedient representative. The reader should evaluate the title against the text.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-high]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) 1 Corinthians 15:45 — "the last Adam became a life-giving spirit." (b) Romans 5:18–19 — the one man's obedience makes the many righteous. (c) 1 Corinthians 15:21–22 — "by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead."
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The "last Adam" could be read as the eschatological Adam rather than the representative head. (b) The parallel could be symbolic rather than structural.
- *Band justification:* The title "last Adam" is explicit; the interpretation as the corresponding human representative is the natural reading of the parallel with the first Adam. `[I-high]` is honest.

## C.9 Pattern of Disobedience

The term "pattern of disobedience" is the project's description of the recurring shape of human rebellion across the canonical story. The text does not use the term; the project uses it as a shorthand for the sequence in Genesis 4–11 (Cain's violence, Lamech's enlarged vengeance, the pre-flood condition, the persistence of evil after the flood, Babel's collective self-exaltation) and the apostolic argument in Romans 1:18–32 (suppressed knowledge, idolatrous exchange, divine handing-over, disordered conduct, social approval of evil). The term is `[I-mid]` for the pattern (the events are explicit; the interpretation as a recurring shape is an inference from the sequence and convergence). The project uses "pattern of disobedience" to describe the human condition the redeemer must address. The reader should evaluate the pattern against the text.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-mid]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) Genesis 4–11 sequences the episodes from individual violence to collective self-exaltation. (b) Romans 1:18–32 orders the chain from suppressed knowledge to social approval of evil. (c) The pattern prepares the need for the obedient representative (Chapters 11–14).
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The texts describe discrete events; the pattern is the chapter's interpretation of the sequence. (b) The pattern could be read as moral observation rather than a constitutional condition.
- *Band justification:* The sequence and convergence are explicit; the interpretation as a recurring pattern is a reasonable inference from the canonical shape. `[I-mid]` is honest.

## C.10 New Humanity

The term "new humanity" is the project's description of the restoration of what was lost in the fall. The text uses "new creation" (2 Corinthians 5:17), "one new man" (Ephesians 2:15), and the new self "created after the likeness of God" (Ephesians 4:24; Colossians 3:10). The term is `[E]` for the textual language; the *interpretation* as the restoration of the image, the relational rupture, and the constitutional condition is `[I-high]` (the parallels with the image of God in Genesis 1:26–28 and the renewal of creation in Romans 8:19–23 are convergent). The project uses "new humanity" as the biblical description of the restored people in the last Adam. The reader should evaluate the language against the text.

**Evidence sketch (`[I-high]`):**
- *Evidence for:* (a) 2 Corinthians 5:17 — "if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation." (b) Ephesians 2:15 — "one new man" in Christ. (c) Ephesians 4:24 and Colossians 3:10 — the new self created after the likeness/image of God, renewed in knowledge. (d) The parallels with the image of God in Genesis 1:26–28 and the renewal of creation in Romans 8:19–23 tie the new humanity to the restoration of what was lost in Chapter 5.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) The "new creation" could be read as individual regeneration rather than cosmic restoration. (b) The "one new man" in Ephesians 2:15 is the Jew-Gentile unity in the church, not the full restoration of the image.
- *Band justification:* The "new creation" and "new man" language is explicit; the restoration of the image and the reversal of the losses of Chapter 5 is the natural reading of the convergent parallels. `[I-high]` is honest.
