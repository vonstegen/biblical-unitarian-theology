# Chapter 11 — Why Must the Redeemer Be Human?

> **Status:** First draft (2026-08-06). Per `01_Architecture_and_Outline.md` (v1.3) and the Introduction §I. The first chapter of Part IV. The chapter's argument is the *correspondence principle* — the redeemer must be *of the same order* as the debtor. The principle is drawn from the Pauline Adam-Christ parallel (Romans 5:12–21; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49) and the explicit Pauline/Hebrew statements about Christ's *humanity* (1 Timothy 2:5; Hebrews 2:14–17). The chapter confines itself to the *textual* evidence; the christological *conclusions* are *drawn from* the *text*, not *imposed* on it.

## 11.1 The question

The chapter's question is the *kind of being* the *redeemer* is. The *kind of being* the redeemer is must *correspond* to the *kind of being* the debtor is — the *correspondence principle*. The chapter argues the *correspondence* is explicit in the *textual* evidence, and the *christological* conclusion is *drawn from* the *correspondence*. The chapter does *not* argue the *redeemer* is *God* in the *Nicene* sense; the chapter argues the *redeemer* is *human* — the *kind of being* the *Adam* kind is — because the *debtor* is *human*. The chapter's *acceptance criterion* is explicit: the chapter answers the *why not a God-man?* question via the *correspondence* principle, drawn from the *Adam-Christ parallels* themselves — no appeal to *outside* categories.

The chapter treats the *correspondence principle* as the *kind of being* the *redeemer* is — the *kind of being* that *corresponds* to the *kind of being* the *debtor* is. The *kind of being* the *debtor* is the *kind of being* the *Adam* kind is (chapters 2–6); the *kind of being* the *redeemer* is the *kind of being* the *Adam* kind is. The two are *kindred* — the *kind of being* the *redeemer* is the *kind of being* the *debtor* is, *in the same order*. The chapter argues the *kindred* is the *correspondence principle*.

## 11.10 The chapter's argument in summary

The chapter's *primary* claim is the *correspondence principle* — the *redeemer* must be *of the same order* as the *debtor*. The textual evidence is the *Romans 5:12–21* parallel (the *Adam* is the *type* of the *one who was to come*), the *1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49* parallel (the *one man* / *one man* structure), the *1 Timothy 2:5* mediator passage (the *mediator* is *the man Christ Jesus*), the *Hebrews 2:14–17* passage (the *redeemer* is *made like his brothers in every respect*), the *Galatians 4:4–5* passage (the *Son* is *born of woman, born under the law*), the *Romans 1:3–4* passage (the *Son* is *descended from David according to the flesh*), the *Philippians 2:6–8* passage (the *Son* is *in the form of God* and *in the form of a servant*), the *Hebrews 4:15* passage (the *high priest* is *tempted in every respect*), and the *Hebrews 5:7–9* passage (the *Son* is *learned obedience*). The chapter rates the *correspondence principle* as `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *kind of being* the *same order* is `[I-high]`.

The chapter's *secondary* claim is the *kind of being* the *redeemer* is the *kind of being* the *human* is — the *kind of being* the *Adam* kind is, the *kind of being* the *descendants* are. The *correspondence* is *kindred* — the *kind of being* the *redeemer* is, the *kind of being* the *debtor* is, *in the same order*. The chapter rates the *kindred* as `[E]` for the textual evidence — the *textual* statements are *explicit* on the *correspondence* side.

The chapter's *negative* claim is the chapter does *not* argue the *redeemer* is *God* in the *Nicene* sense. The *kind of being* the *redeemer* is, the *kind of being* the *human* is, *in the same order* as the *debtor*. The chapter treats the *kind of being* the *redeemer* is the *kind of being* the *human* is — the *kind of being* the *redeemer* is, the *kind of being* the *human* is, *in the same order*. The chapter confines itself to the *textual* evidence; the *christological* *conclusions* (the *Nicene* / *Chalcedonian* posture) are *drawn from* the *text*, not *imposed* on it. The chapter's *forward* reference is the *kind of being* the *redeemer* is the *kind of being* the *obedience* is (Chapter 12) and the *kind of being* the *redeemer* is the *kind of being* the *willing* is (Chapter 13, the *Gethsemane* passage).
