# Chapter 11 — Why Must the Redeemer Be Human?

> **Status:** Paired foundation revision (2026-08-07). Chapter 11 is the prior load-bearing chapter for Part IV: it establishes the human debtor, the human penalty, and the same-order correspondence principle. Chapters 11 and 15 are co-equal load-bearing chapters; Chapter 15 cannot be accepted until Chapter 11 passes the A11 gate. This revision strengthens the debt-side foundation and pressure-tests the principal counter-readings while keeping the christological conclusion as a result of the exegesis.

## 11.1 The question

Chapters 4–6 established the debt-debtor structure: Adam's disobedience incurred the obligation the manuscript calls a debt; death was declared in Eden and executed in the human creature (Ch 3 §§3.2–3.4; Ch 6 §§6.2–6.3). Chapter 7 added that the debtor class passes through a developmental arc — from the constitutional inheritance received in Adam to the forensic accountability of the adult — without specifying the age at which the latter arrives. The remaining question is what sort of representative can correspond to the debtor and pay the obligation the first debtor incurred.

**Debt-side foundation: the penalty belongs to the human debtor.** The correspondence question rests on a prior textual fact, not on an abstract analogy. God addresses the command to "the man" and declares its consequence in human terms: "in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die" (Genesis 2:16–17, ESV; verified against esv.org on 2026-08-07) `[E]`. Genesis 3:17–19 records the execution of that sentence in the same human creature: Adam is returned to the ground from which he was taken, "for you are dust, and to dust you shall return" (ESV; verified against esv.org on 2026-08-07) `[E]`. The penalty is therefore not an undefined defect in creation; it is the declared consequence of a human act under a command given to a human debtor.

Paul gives the judicial frame for that event. "Sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all men" (Romans 5:12, ESV; verified against esv.org on 2026-08-07) `[E]`. He restates the same order in resurrection language: "as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead" and "as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive" (1 Corinthians 15:21–22, ESV; verified against esv.org on 2026-08-07) `[E]`. Scripture's load-bearing terms in this sequence are command, sin, death, condemnation, and the human representative. The manuscript's word *debt* is a summary label for that declared obligation and penalty, not a claim that Romans 5 uses commercial terminology here `[I-mid]`.

**Evidence sketch — `[I-high]` "The human debt requires a corresponding human representative"**
- *Evidence for:* (a) Genesis 2:16–17 addresses "the man" and assigns death as the consequence of his disobedience. (b) Genesis 3:17–19 executes that consequence in the human debtor's return to dust. (c) Romans 5:12 locates sin and death's entry in one man, and Romans 5:18–19 places one man's obedience opposite one man's trespass. (d) 1 Corinthians 15:21–22 repeats the man : man correspondence in the movement from death to resurrection.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* Genesis 2:16–17 does not state in a separate proposition that a future redeemer must bear the penalty; the necessity is inferred from the penalty's human addressee and Paul's later man-to-man parallel. The texts therefore establish the debtor's order and the correspondence pattern more directly than they state the formula "pure human for pure human."
- *Band justification:* The human addressee, human execution of the penalty, and repeated Pauline man : man structure make corresponding human representation the strongest reading; because no single passage states the rule in those exact words, `[I-high]` rather than `[E]` is honest.

This subsection secures the prior condition for the rest of Part IV. It identifies the order of the debtor and the penalty that must be answered; it does not yet explain why one corresponding human's obedience can affect the many. That numerical and representative mechanism is Chapter 15's task, and Chapter 15 must inherit this foundation rather than assume it silently.

The correspondence principle — that the redeemer must stand in the same order as the debtor — is the chapter's claim. The chapter draws the correspondence principle from the Pauline Adam-Christ parallel and from the explicit New Testament statements about Christ's humanity. The redeemer must be what the debtor is: a human being in the same order of existence. The acceptance criterion (A11) is whether the chapter answers *why not a God-man?* via the correspondence principle, drawn from the texts themselves, with no appeal to Nicene or Chalcedonian categories as theological authority.

## 11.2 The Adam-Christ parallel: same order, opposite acts

The foundational text is Romans 5:18–19:

> "Therefore, as one trespass led to condemnation for all men, so one act of righteousness leads to justification and life for all men. For as by the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners, so by the one man's obedience the many will be made righteous." (Romans 5:18–19, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]` — E11.1

The structure is explicit. The parallel has two axes. The numerical axis (one / one) is carried by Romans 5:18 itself: *di' henos paraptōmatos* ("through one trespass") is set over against *di' henos dikaiōmatos* ("through one act of righteousness") — neuter *henos* (one) agreeing with each act, not with a man. The categorical axis (man / man) is supplied by the wider Romans 5 unit rather than by duplicated *anthrōpos* in 5:18 or 5:19: Romans 5:12 says "sin came into the world through one man" (*di' henos anthrōpou*, the only place that exact construction appears), and Romans 5:19a says "by the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners" (*tou henos anthrōpou*); the obedience side of 5:19 reads "by the obedience of the one" (*tou henos*, without *anthrōpos*). The man : man correspondence therefore rests on (i) the 5:12 + 5:19a Adam-side *anthrōpos* anchors, (ii) the verse-internal *through the one man* / *through the obedience of the one* symmetry in 5:19, and (iii) Paul's own man : man parallel in 1 Corinthians 15:21 ("by a man... by a man") and the title-pair *first man* / *last Adam* / *second man* in 1 Corinthians 15:45, 47. Both sides of the parallel are therefore named by their humanity `[E]`, and the inference that the parallel itself demands sameness of order is `[I-high]`.

Paul extends the parallel in 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45, 47:

> "For as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive." (1 Corinthians 15:21–22, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

> "Thus it is written, 'The first man Adam became a living being'; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit... The first man was from the earth, a man of dust; the second man is from heaven." (1 Corinthians 15:45, 47, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]` — E11.2

The titles are decisive for the textual structure. Adam is the first man; Christ is the last Adam and the second man. The counterpart to the first man is another man. A representative of a different order would break the parallel Paul constructs. The parallel is man : man, not an unnamed representative of a different order. `[E]` for the man / man structure and the last Adam / second man titles; `[I-high]` for the inference that the parallel itself demands sameness of order.

**Evidence sketch — `[I-high]` "The Adam-Christ parallel demands a redeemer of the same order as the debtor"**
- *Evidence for:* (a) Romans 5:12 supplies *di' henos anthrōpou* ("through one man," Adam) and Romans 5:19a supplies *tou henos anthrōpou* ("of the one man," Adam) — these are the two places in Romans 5 where *henos anthrōpos* is stated explicitly, both on the Adam side `[E]`. (b) Romans 5:19 sets *through the one man's disobedience* over against *through the obedience of the one*, and the symmetry names Christ as the counterpart representative of the Adam figure; the Christ side of 5:19 names *the one* rather than *the one man*, so the man-side of 5:19 is carried by the Adam clause and the unit context. (c) Romans 5:14 calls Adam "a type of the one who was to come" (Greek *tupos*); that typological pairing is consistent with the same-order reading, although the verse does not state a general rule that every type and antitype must share the same kind. (d) 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45, 47 carries the explicit man : man language ("by a man... by a man," "first man Adam," "last Adam," "second man"); *deuteros anthrōpos* identifies the category used in that parallel.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* (a) 1 Corinthians 15:47 calls Christ the second man from heaven; the qualifier does not appear on Adam's side and could be read as a categorical asymmetry. (b) The last Adam became a life-giving spirit (15:45), a description Adam does not receive; the spirit-language could be read as a difference of order rather than role.
- *Band justification:* The repeated man / man and first-man / second-man structure makes same-order correspondence the strongest reading, while the heaven and spirit qualifiers leave a real alternative reading open. `[I-high]` is therefore appropriate for the inference, not `[E]`.

## 11.3 The parallel runs in both directions

The one-man / one-man structure of the Adam-Christ parallel is symmetrical, and the symmetrical reading constrains both sides. Paul names the first figure the first man Adam and a man of dust; he names the second the last Adam and the second man (1 Corinthians 15:45, 47). The titles use the same noun — man — for both. A representative of a different or mixed order on the second side would require the parallel's first side to be of that different or mixed order as well, or the one-for-one correspondence Paul constructs would no longer be intact.

Scripture never describes Adam as anything other than a man. His name (Hebrew *adam*) means man / human; in Genesis 2–3 the figure who receives the command, disobeys, and incurs the death-penalty is the first human adam formed from the ground. Romans 5 calls him the one man whose trespass condemned the many; 1 Corinthians 15 calls him the first man, a man of dust. The first side of the parallel is, by explicit textual testimony, plainly human.

This is the strict logic of sameness of order. If the Redeemer were a representative of a mixed or different order, the parallel would no longer be man : man. Adam was plainly a man. Therefore the conclusion defended here is that the Redeemer is a pure-human representative of the same order as Adam, not a representative whose decisive redemptive work depends on a different order. The debt was incurred by a man under terms given to a man; the payment must be rendered by a man standing in the relation the parallel specifies. The negative case (*why not a God-man?*) is therefore an inference from the bidirectional structure, not an imported conclusion. `[I-high]`

The asymmetrical qualifiers in the Corinthians passage must be faced rather than ignored. "From heaven" appears on Christ's side, but the immediate context still calls him the second man and then says that those who belong to him will bear the image of the man of heaven (1 Corinthians 15:47–49). The phrase therefore supplies an origin and an eschatological identity within Paul's man-to-man pairing; it does not explicitly say that the second man is not human. Likewise, "life-giving spirit" follows the contrast between the natural and the spiritual (15:45–46) and describes the life-giving role of the risen Christ. That is the strongest contextual reading, but a reader may still treat the qualifiers as marking a categorical asymmetry. `[I-high]`

**Evidence sketch — `[I-high]` "The Corinthians counter-texts do not overturn the same-order conclusion"**
- *Evidence for:* (a) Paul repeats *man* in "by a man" / "by a man" (15:21) and names Christ "the second man" (15:47). (b) The "from heaven" statement is immediately followed by the image-bearing correspondence of those who are of heaven (15:48–49). (c) The "life-giving spirit" description is set inside the natural-to-spiritual sequence (15:45–46), which explains function and condition rather than expressly denying humanity.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* The heaven qualifier is absent from Adam's description, and the life-giving-spirit language is not applied to Adam. Those asymmetries can support a reading in which Christ has a categorical distinction beyond the human order.
- *Band justification:* The repeated man-language and immediate image parallel make origin-and-role the strongest reading of the qualifiers, but the asymmetry is genuine and no verse states the exclusion in propositional form. `[I-high]` is honest.

## 11.4 "The man Christ Jesus" as mediator

> "For there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, who gave himself as a ransom for all..." (1 Timothy 2:5–6, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]` — E11.3

The verse explicitly states that there is one God, one mediator, and that the mediator is "the man Christ Jesus" who gives himself as a ransom `[E]`. The further conclusion that this wording excludes every composite account is an inference from the text's role assignment, not a claim made by silence: the passage distinguishes God from the mediator and assigns the ransom to the mediator-man. `[I-mid]`

**Evidence sketch — `[I-mid]` "First Timothy 2:5 identifies the ransom mediator as the man Christ Jesus"**
- *Evidence for:* The verse's explicit one-God / one-mediator structure and its direct naming of the mediator as *anthrōpos Christos Iēsous* (1 Timothy 2:5–6).
- *Evidence against / weakening:* The passage does not expressly state that the mediator has no additional status or qualification; the exclusion of a composite reading cannot be proved from silence alone.
- *Band justification:* The human identity and mediating role are explicit, while the exclusionary implication is contextual. `[I-mid]` keeps those two claims distinct.

## 11.5 Shared flesh and blood (Hebrews 2:14–17)

> "Since therefore the children share in flesh and blood, he himself likewise partook of the same things, that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil, and deliver all those who through fear of death were subject to lifelong slavery. For surely it is not angels that he helps, but he helps the offspring of Abraham. Therefore he had to be made like his brothers in every respect, so that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest in the service of God, to make propitiation for the sins of the people." (Hebrews 2:14–17, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]` — E11.4

The argument is causal. The children share in flesh and blood; therefore he himself likewise partook of the same things. The purpose clauses are explicit: that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death, and so that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest. He had to be made like his brothers in every respect. The had to be made language is necessity — the high-priestly work could not be performed otherwise.

The text rules out angelic mediation (not angels that he helps, but he helps the offspring of Abraham) and grounds both the death and the high-priestly work in shared human nature. A being who did not share flesh and blood could neither die the death that frees those enslaved by fear of death, nor stand as the merciful high priest who makes propitiation for the people. The Greek *koinōnein* ("to share in / partake of") and the *tōn autōn* ("the same things") name a real participation, not a nominal one. `[E]`

## 11.6 Born of woman, born under the law (Galatians 4:4–5)

> "But when the fullness of time had come, God sent forth his Son, born of woman, born under the law, to redeem those who were under the law, so that we might receive adoption as sons." (Galatians 4:4–5, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The text explicitly states that the Son was born of woman, born under the law, and sent to redeem those under the law `[E]`. Its purpose clause links the Redeemer's entry into those conditions with the redemption of those who occupy them. The conclusion that the correspondence is therefore necessary — that the redeemer must answer a human and legal condition from within that condition — is `[I-high]`, not an additional explicit quotation.

**Evidence sketch — `[I-high]` "Galatians 4:4–5 presents shared human and legal condition as necessary to the redemption described"**
- *Evidence for:* (a) "Born of woman" and "born under the law" identify the conditions entered. (b) "To redeem those who were under the law" supplies the purpose clause. (c) Hebrews 2:17 uses stronger necessity language — "he had to be made like his brothers in every respect" — for the corresponding priestly work.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* Galatians 4:4–5 does not formulate the rule as "a redeemer must be human"; the necessity is derived from the purpose and the parallel with Hebrews 2, not stated as an abstract proposition.
- *Band justification:* The purpose clause and the Hebrews parallel make shared condition the strongest reading of the passage's redemptive logic, but the exact necessity formula remains inferential. `[I-high]` is honest.

## 11.7 Descended from David according to the flesh (Romans 1:3–4)

> "Concerning his Son, who was descended from David according to the flesh and was declared to be the Son of God in power according to the Spirit of holiness by his resurrection from the dead, Jesus Christ our Lord." (Romans 1:3–4, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The text explicitly gives two identifications: descent from David "according to the flesh" and declaration as Son of God in power "according to the Spirit of holiness" by resurrection `[E]`. This chapter reads the two clauses as lineage and resurrection-vindication rather than as a statement of two natures `[I-mid]`. The first clause places the Redeemer inside Israel's human history; the second describes God's public act concerning him. Appendix B addresses the wider range of readings for high-Christology texts without using one reading as an external authority.

**Evidence sketch — `[I-mid]` "Romans 1:3–4 is best read here as two registers of identification"**
- *Evidence for:* The verse itself contrasts "according to the flesh" with "according to the Spirit of holiness" and locates the second declaration at resurrection. The surrounding Pauline argument repeatedly treats resurrection as God's act concerning Jesus.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* The contrast can be read by another interpreter as a distinction of natures rather than registers; Romans 1:3–4 does not explicitly deny that reading.
- *Band justification:* The lineage-plus-resurrection sequence supports the register reading in this chapter, but the verse's compact contrast leaves the alternative open. `[I-mid]` is the appropriate band.

## 11.8 Form of a servant, likeness of men (Philippians 2:6–8)

> "Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross." (Philippians 2:6–8, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The passage explicitly contrasts "form of God" with "form of a servant," "likeness of men," and "human form," and it explicitly locates the obedience in the path to death `[E]`. The meaning of *morphē theou* and *equality with God* is not settled here; Appendix B §B.3 presents the competing readings of Philippians 2:6–11. The present chapter's load-bearing point is narrower: the redemptive obedience and death are narrated through the servant who is born in the likeness of men and found in human form.

The human terms are concrete. *Homoiōmati anthrōpōn* ("likeness of men") names the human category Paul uses in this sentence, and *schēmati hōs anthrōpos* ("in human form / found in human form") describes the form in which the Redeemer is found. The inference that these terms represent genuine, not nominal, entry into the human order is `[I-high]`; it is supported by the parallel's death, obedience, and the shared-condition texts in Hebrews 2 and 4, but the verse does not itself use the phrase "pure human."

**Evidence sketch — `[I-high]` "Philippians 2:7–8 presents the decisive obedience as genuinely human"**
- *Evidence for:* (a) The passage says "being born in the likeness of men" and "found in human form." (b) The same subject becomes obedient to death, connecting the human form to the decisive act. (c) Hebrews 2:14–17 and 4:15–5:9 independently connect death, temptation, and obedience with shared flesh and human experience.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* The opening "form of God" and "equality with God" language can support a reading in which a higher-order status remains essential to the subject. Appendix B §B.3 records that alternative rather than treating it as resolved by this chapter.
- *Band justification:* The human-side terms explicitly ground the obedience in human form, while the opening terms leave a competing interpretation available. `[I-high]` is strong for the redemptive action's human register without pretending that Philippians 2:6 is textually silent.

## 11.9 Tempted as we are; learned obedience through suffering (Hebrews 4:15; 5:7–9)

> "For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin." (Hebrews 4:15, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

> "In the days of his flesh, Jesus offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears, to him who was able to save him from death, and he was heard because of his reverence. Although he was a son, he learned obedience through what he suffered. And being made perfect, he became the source of eternal salvation to all who obey him." (Hebrews 5:7–9, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The high priest is able to sympathize because he has been tempted in every respect as we are. He learned obedience through what he suffered. These statements presuppose a genuine human experience of testing, suffering, and growth in obedience. The Greek *peirazein* ("to tempt / test") used in 4:15 is the same verb used in the temptation of Jesus (Matt 4; Luke 4) — the in every respect (*kata panta*) names a complete correspondence of testing, not a partial one.

The in the days of his flesh (5:7) frames the obedience within the human order. A figure who did not share that order of existence could not be said to have learned obedience through suffering or to have been tempted as we are. The Greek *emathen hupakoēn* ("learned obedience") names a developmental progression; the through what he suffered (*aph' hōn epathen*) names the mechanism. The yet without sin (Heb 4:15) is the qualification — the obedience is real obedience rather than simulated obedience; the sinlessness is the match to the requirement, not the substitution of one order for another.

This is also where the chapter's argument integrates with Ch 7. Chapter 7 observed that the human being is constitutionally bent under Adam but is developmentally worked through — a human being who is tested, who learns, who grows. The inference that correspondence therefore includes the Redeemer's full participation in that developmental human arc is `[I-high]`: Hebrews says he was tempted as we are, lived in the days of his flesh, and learned obedience through suffering. The text does not present a formal theory of "human order," so the chapter states the integration as an inference rather than as `[E]`.

**Evidence sketch — `[I-high]` "The corresponding representative enters the human arc of testing and obedience"**
- *Evidence for:* Hebrews 4:15 says "in every respect" and "as we are"; Hebrews 5:7–9 places the experience "in the days of his flesh" and says he learned obedience through suffering; the sinlessness qualifies the success of that human obedience.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* The Hebrews texts describe the experience and its result but do not state the broader category formula that this developmental arc is what defines the required order.
- *Band justification:* The convergent human-experience language makes full participation the strongest reading, while the category itself remains an inference. `[I-high]` is honest.

## 11.10 Synthesis: correspondence requires full humanity

The texts converge on a single structural requirement.

- The first man brought death; the counterpart is also a man (Romans 5:18–19; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45, 47).
- The one mediator between God and men is the man Christ Jesus (1 Timothy 2:5–6).
- He partook of the same flesh and blood as the children, that through death he might deliver them and so that he might become a merciful high priest (Hebrews 2:14–17).
- He was born of woman, born under the law — to redeem those under the law (Galatians 4:4–5).
- He is descended from David according to the flesh (Romans 1:3–4).
- He took the form of a servant, was born in the likeness of men, and was found in human form (Philippians 2:6–8).
- He was tempted in every respect as we are and learned obedience through what he suffered (Hebrews 4:15; 5:7–9).

From these statements the chapter draws the correspondence principle `[I-high]`. The debtor is human; the debt is the debt of human disobedience under the terms God established for humanity. The one who repays must stand in that same order. A representative whose decisive redemptive work depended on a different order would not occupy the place the man-to-man parallel specifies, would not answer the shared-flesh and under-law conditions attached to redemption, and would not render the obedience the texts describe as the path of the Redeemer. The phrase *pure human* therefore names the order and agency of the representative's redemptive work; it is a conclusion from the converging texts, not a premise imported before them.

**Evidence sketch — `[I-high]` "The correspondence principle rules out a different-order representative as the basis of payment"**
- *Evidence for:* (a) Romans 5:18–19 and 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49 repeatedly pair man with man. (b) Hebrews 2:14–17 makes shared flesh and likeness to the brothers necessary to death and priestly propitiation. (c) Galatians 4:4–5 connects birth under the law with redemption of those under the law. (d) Hebrews 4:15–5:9 grounds the obedience in human testing, suffering, and flesh.
- *Evidence against / weakening:* The passages do not state in one sentence that no additional status or qualifier can coexist with the human representative; Philippians 2:6 and 1 Corinthians 15:45, 47 contain the principal asymmetrical language. Appendix B presents those alternative readings. The chapter's claim is therefore about what order performs the decisive redemptive work, not a claim that every difficult christological text has been resolved here.
- *Band justification:* The clear man-to-man and shared-condition passages make the pure-human representative reading the strongest account of the payment logic; the counter-texts and absence of a single propositional formula keep the claim at `[I-high]` rather than `[E]`.

The New Testament's explicit descriptions assign the decisive work to the man who is the last Adam, the second man, the mediator, the one made like his brothers, and the one who learned obedience in the days of his flesh. On the reading defended here, those descriptions require a pure-human representative; they do not require a second order to perform the payment. `[I-high]`

**Translation notes:**
- Romans 5:18–19 — the 5:18 Greek is *di' henos paraptōmatos* and *di' henos dikaiōmatos*, where *henos* is neuter and agrees with the acts, not with a man. The 5:19a Greek carries *tou henos anthrōpou* on the Adam side ("of the one man") and *tou henos* on the obedience side ("of the one"), so the man : man correspondence in Romans 5 rests on 5:12 (*di' henos anthrōpou*, Adam) and 5:19a (*tou henos anthrōpou*, Adam), reinforced by the verse-internal *through the one man* / *through the obedience of the one* symmetry in 5:19 and by 1 Corinthians 15:21, 45, 47. The man : man pairing is therefore not carried by a duplicated *anthrōpos* on every clause in Romans 5:18–19. *Paraptōma* (trespass) and *dikaiōma* (righteous act / judicial decision) preserve the one / one parallel across the translation set; the precise shade of *dikaiōma* remains contested.
- Philippians 2:6–7 — *morphē theou* (form of God) and *kenoō* (emptied himself) are left open at the metaphysical level; Appendix B §B.3 records the competing readings. The present chapter relies on the explicit servant, likeness-of-men, human-form, obedience, and death language.
- Hebrews 2:16 — *epilambanomai* (take hold of / help) and *sperma Abraam* (offspring of Abraham) locate the help on the Abrahamic-descendant side.
- Hebrews 5:8 — *manthanō* (learn) and the phrase "through what he suffered" support the developmental description of the obedience without turning it into a premise about metaphysics.
- Genesis 2–3 — *adam* (man / human / mankind) is both the generic term and the personal name of the first man formed from the ground; the chapter uses the explicit narrative and Pauline *one man* language rather than treating the lexical connection alone as proof.
