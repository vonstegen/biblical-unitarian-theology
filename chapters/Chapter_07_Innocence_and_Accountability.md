## 7.1 The question

The chapter's question is whether *childhood* is a real moral category in the biblical text. The question is *not* whether children are *innocent* in the *constitutional* sense (the *constitutional* transmission is the topic of Chapter 6); the question is whether *childhood* is a real moral category in the *forensic* sense — whether the *developmental* stage of being a child is a *kind* of being that is *not* morally accountable in the way adults are.

The chapter's argument is *not* the *age of accountability* claim (the *systematic-theological* synthesis that children are *innocent* until a specific age). The chapter's argument is more *modest*: the *developmental* stage of being a child is a *kind* of being that the *text* treats as *distinct* from the *moral* accountability of the *adult*. The chapter does *not* attempt to specify the *age* at which the *kind* of being the child is becomes the *kind* of being the adult is. The chapter confines itself to the *textual* evidence that the *kind* of being the child is *distinct* from the *kind* of being the adult is.

The chapter's *acceptance criterion* is explicit: the *innocence* is meaningful in Scripture *without* committing to a specific age. The chapter refrains from over-claiming.

## 7.2 The Deuteronomy 1:39 evidence

The Deuteronomy passage is the *explicit* textual evidence that the *children who have not known good and evil* are a *kind* of being that is *distinct* from the moral accountability of the adults.

> "And as for your little ones, who you said would become a prey, and your children, who today have no knowledge of good or evil, they shall go in there. And to them I will give it, and they shall possess it." (Deuteronomy 1:39, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is the *post-Spy-Report* narrative. The twelve spies have returned from Canaan; ten have brought a *bad report*; the people have *murmured* against Moses; the divine punishment is the *forty years of wilderness wandering*. The verse is the *limit* of the punishment: the *little ones* who *have no knowledge of good or evil* are *excluded* from the punishment. The *exclusion* is the *moral* category: the children are *not* part of the *murmuring* generation because they are *not* morally accountable for the murmuring. The *no knowledge of good or evil* is the *developmental* description — the *kind* of being the children are is the *kind* of being that does not know good and evil.

The chapter reads the verse as the *explicit* textual evidence that the *kind* of being the child is (the *kind* of being without *knowledge of good or evil*) is the *kind* of being that is *not* morally accountable. The *exclusion* from the punishment is the *forensic* confirmation: the children are *not* part of the *murmuring* generation's judgment. `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *moral* category (rather than the *biological* category) is `[I-high]` (the textual evidence is the *exclusion*; the *interpretation* as the *moral* distinction is the parallel reading).

## 7.3 The Isaiah 7:15–16 evidence

The Isaiah passage is the *explicit* textual evidence that the *child knowing to refuse evil* is a *developmental* milestone — the *kind* of being the child is becomes the *kind* of being the adult is.

> "He shall eat curds and honey when he knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good. For before the boy knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good, the land whose two kings you dread will be deserted." (Isaiah 7:15–16, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is the *Immanuel* prophecy. The *child* is the *sign* of the *Immanuel* — the *child* grows up to *know* to refuse the evil and choose the good. The *developmental* description is the *kind* of being the child is — the *kind* of being that does not yet *know* to refuse the evil. The *milestone* of *knowing* to refuse the evil is the *transition* from the *child* to the *adult* — the developmental *kind* of being.

The chapter reads the verse as the *explicit* textual evidence that the *kind* of being the child is (the *kind* of being that does not yet *know* to refuse the evil) is the *kind* of being that is *distinct* from the *kind* of being the adult is (the *kind* of being that *knows* to refuse the evil). The *developmental* description is the *kind* of being the child is; the *moral* distinction is the *kind* of being the adult is. `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *moral* category (rather than the *biological* category) is `[I-high]`.

## 7.4 The Jonah 4:11 evidence

The Jonah passage is the *explicit* textual evidence that the *kind of being the child is* is the *kind* of being that does not yet *know* the *moral* distinction.

> "And should not I pity Nineveh, that great city, in which there are more than 120,000 persons who do not know their right hand from their left, and also much cattle?" (Jonah 4:11, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is the *closing* of the book of Jonah. The *Ninevites* are *described* as 120,000 persons who *do not know their right hand from their left*. The phrase is the *kind* of being the Ninevites are — the *kind* of being without *moral* knowledge. The phrase is sometimes read as *infants* (the literal *children*); the chapter reads the phrase as *the kind of being without moral knowledge* — the *kind* of being that does not yet *know* the *moral* distinction. The reading is `[I-mid]` (the textual evidence is the *phrase*; the *interpretation* as the *moral* category is the chapter's reading; the *literal* reading of *infants* is the alternative).

The chapter's argument is *not* that the *Ninevites* are *infants*; the chapter's argument is that the *Ninevites* are *the kind of being without moral knowledge* — the *kind* of being that is *pitied* because they are *that kind of being*. The *pity* is the *moral* category: the *pity* is *for* the *kind of being* that the Ninevites are, not *for* the *moral* guilt that the Ninevites have. The chapter reads the *pity* as the *forensic* confirmation: the *kind of being the child is* is the *kind of being* that is *pitied* because they are *that kind of being*, not morally accountable for the *adult* kind of being. `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *moral* category is `[I-high]`.

## 7.5 The Romans 9:11 evidence

The Romans passage is the *explicit* textual evidence that the *kind of being the child is* is the *kind of being* that is *not* yet morally accountable in the *adult* sense.

> "Though they were not yet born and had done nothing either good or bad — in order that God's purpose of election might continue, not because of works but because of him who calls" (Romans 9:11, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is the *Jacob-and-Esau* passage. The *kind of being* the *not yet born* children are is the *kind of being* that does not yet *do* anything either good or bad. The *before* the *moral* action is the *developmental* milestone — the *kind of being* that is *before* the *moral* action is the *kind of being* that is *not* morally accountable for the *action* they have not yet done. The verse's argument is *election*, but the *developmental* description is the *kind of being* the children are.

The chapter reads the verse as the *explicit* textual evidence that the *kind of being the child is* (the *kind of being* before the *moral* action) is the *kind of being* that is *not* morally accountable in the *adult* sense. The *before* the *moral* action is the *developmental* description — the *kind of being* that is *distinct* from the *kind of being* that *does* the moral action. `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *moral* category is `[I-high]`.

## 7.6 The 2 Timothy 3:15 evidence

The 2 Timothy passage is the *explicit* textual evidence that the *child* is *trained* in the *scriptures* — the *kind of being* the child is becomes the *kind of being* the adult is through *training*.

> "And how from childhood you have been acquainted with the sacred writings, which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus." (2 Timothy 3:15, ESV; verified against esv.org) `[E]`

The verse is the *Paul-to-Timothy* instruction. The *childhood* is the *developmental* stage of being *acquainted* with the *scriptures*. The *making wise* is the *transition* from the *child* to the *adult* — the *kind of being* the *acquainted* child is becomes the *kind of being* the *wise* adult is. The *training* is the *moral* category: the *training* is the *kind of being* the *developmental* adult is — the *kind* of being that *knows* the *moral* distinction.

The chapter reads the verse as the *explicit* textual evidence that the *kind of being the child is* is the *kind of being* that is *trained* to become the *kind of being* the adult is. The *training* is the *moral* category: the *training* is the *developmental* transition from the *child* to the *adult*. The chapter treats the *training* as the *kind* of being that the *moral* instructions in the *scriptures* are *for*. The *kind of being* that is *trained* is the *kind of being* that is *not yet* morally accountable in the *forensic* sense; the *kind of being* that has *been trained* is the *kind of being* that is morally accountable for the *foregoing* training. `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *moral* category is `[I-high]`.

## 7.7 The "age of accountability" question

The chapter treats the *age of accountability* as a *systematic-theological* synthesis that the *text* does not explicitly endorse. The chapter's argument is the *kind* of being the child is (the *developmental* category); the *systematic-theological* synthesis is the *specific age* at which the *kind of being* the child is becomes the *kind of being* the adult is. The chapter refrains from the *systematic-theological* synthesis.

The textual evidence is *not* explicit on the *specific age*. The Deuteronomy 1:39 passage specifies *children who have no knowledge of good or evil* — the *developmental* description, not the *age*. The Isaiah 7:15–16 passage specifies *the child* knowing to refuse the evil and choose the good — the *developmental* description, not the *age*. The Jonah 4:11 passage specifies *the kind of being* that does not *know* their right hand from their left — the *developmental* description, not the *age*. The Romans 9:11 passage specifies *before they had done anything either good or bad* — the *developmental* description, not the *age*. The 2 Timothy 3:15 passage specifies *from childhood* — the *developmental* description, not the *age*.

The chapter treats the *age of accountability* as `[H]` — the *hypothesis* is plausible but the *text* does not specify the *age*. The *systematic tradition* has proposed various ages (the *Bar Mitzvah* age of 13 in the Jewish tradition; the *Confirmable* age in the Catholic tradition; the *Profession of Faith* age in the Reformed tradition; the *syllable* age in the Quaker tradition). The chapter does not endorse any specific age; the chapter endorses the *developmental* category without specifying the *age* at which the *transition* occurs.

**`[H-high]` evidence sketch — "The biblical text supports an 'age of accountability' at which children become morally accountable adults"**

- *Evidence for:* (a) The Deuteronomy 1:39, Isaiah 7:15–16, Jonah 4:11, Romans 9:11, and 2 Timothy 3:15 passages all support the *developmental* category — the *kind of being the child is* is *distinct* from the *kind of being the adult is*. (b) The *systematic tradition* has proposed various ages; the *ages* are the *natural* readings of the *developmental* descriptions. (c) The *moral* category of children is *real* in the *forensic* sense — the children are *not* part of the *murmuring* generation (Deuteronomy 1:39); the children are *pitied* as the *kind of being* they are (Jonah 4:11). (d) The *forensic* distinction is *real* — the *son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father* (Ezekiel 18:20); the *kind of being the child is* is the *kind* of being that is *not* morally accountable in the *adult* sense.
- *Evidence against:* (a) The *age* is *not* specified in any of the *textual* passages — the *developmental* descriptions are *kind-of-being* descriptions, not *age* specifications. (b) The *systematic-theological* tradition has not converged on a single age; the *ages* proposed are *systematic* inferences, not *textual* statements. (c) The *age of accountability* may be a *cultural* synthesis (the *Bar Mitzvah* / *Confirmation* age) that the *text* does not explicitly endorse. (d) The *kind of being* the *child* is may be *gradually* transitional — the *developmental* milestones are *real* but the *single-age* transition is a *systematic* oversimplification.
- *Band justification:* The *developmental* category is *strong* (`[E]`); the *specific age* is *hypothesis* (`[H]`); the *chapter's argument* does not depend on the *specific age*. The chapter treats the *age of accountability* claim as `[H-high]` (the *kind of being* the *child* is *real*; the *age* is *not specified*).

## 7.8 The "children knowing good and evil" frame

The *developmental* description is the *kind of being* the child is — the *kind of being* that does not *know* to refuse the evil and choose the good. The *knowing* is the *developmental* transition from the *child* to the *adult*. The chapter treats the *knowing* as the *moral* category — the *kind of being* that *knows* is the *kind of being* that is morally accountable.

The *knowing* is the *textual* evidence for the *moral* category. The *knowing* is *not* a *biological* category (the *age* of the *knowing*); the *knowing* is a *developmental* category (the *kind of being* that knows). The *age* at which the *kind of being* knows is *variable* — the *developmental* milestones are *real* but the *single-age* transition is *not* specified in the *text*.

The chapter treats the *moral* category as the *kind of being* the *knowing* adult is. The *knowing* adult is the *kind of being* that is morally accountable — the *kind of being* that *does* the moral action. The *not-yet-knowing* child is the *kind of being* that is *not* morally accountable in the *adult* sense — the *kind of being* that does *not yet* do the moral action.

The chapter treats the *moral* category as *gradual* rather than *sudden*. The *child* becomes the *adult* as the *developmental* milestones are *reached* — the *knowing* is the *gradual* acquisition of the *moral* category. The *forensic* accountability is the *sudden* imposition of the *moral* category — the *forensic* accountability is the *forensic* judgment that the *adult* is *responsible* for the *foregoing* training. The chapter treats the *gradual* / *sudden* distinction as the *temporal* category — the *gradual* is the *developmental*; the *sudden* is the *forensic*. `[E]` for the *gradual* / *sudden* distinction; the *interpretation* as the *moral* category is `[I-high]`.

## 7.9 The chapter's argument in summary

The chapter's *primary* claim is the *kind of being the child is* is the *kind of being* that is *distinct* from the *kind of being* the adult is. The textual evidence is Deuteronomy 1:39, Isaiah 7:15–16, Jonah 4:11, Romans 9:11, and 2 Timothy 3:15. The chapter rates the *kind of being* the child is as `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *moral* category is `[I-high]`.

The chapter's *secondary* claim is the *developmental* transition from the *child* to the *adult* is the *gradual* acquisition of the *moral* category. The *moral* category is the *kind of being* that *knows* to refuse the evil and choose the good; the *moral* category is the *kind of being* that is *forensically* accountable for the *moral* action. The chapter treats the *gradual* transition as `[I-high]` (the *textual* evidence is the *developmental* descriptions; the *interpretation* as the *gradual* transition is the chapter's reading).

The chapter's *negative* claim is the *age of accountability* is *not* a *textual* claim. The *age* is a *systematic-theological* synthesis; the *text* does not specify the *age*. The chapter treats the *age of accountability* as `[H-high]` (the *kind of being* the child is *real*; the *age* is *not specified*).

The chapter's *positive* claim is the *moral* category is *real* in the *forensic* sense. The *son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father* (Ezekiel 18:20) is the *forensic* limit on the *constitutional* transmission. The *moral* category is the *kind of being* that is *forensically* accountable — the *kind of being* the *developmental* adult is. The chapter treats the *moral* category as `[E]` for the *forensic* limit; the *interpretation* as the *transition* is `[I-high]`.

The chapter's *forward* reference is the *developmental* description of the *child* — the *kind of being* the *child* is — as the *kind of being* that the *Last Adam* (Chapters 11–14) is *for*. The *Last Adam* is the *kind of being* that is the *restoration* of the *kind of being* the *descendant* is to be. The *kind of being* the *Last Adam* is does *not* need the *training* — the *kind of being* the *Last Adam* is is the *kind of being* that *knows* to refuse the evil and choose the good, from the *beginning*. The chapter's *forward* reference is the *developmental* versus *innate* distinction — the *Last Adam* is *innate* (the *kind of being* that *knows*); the *descendant* is *developmental* (the *kind of being* that *learns*).
