## 14.9 The chapter's argument in summary

The chapter's *primary* claim is the *cross* is the *kind of being* the *payment* is — the *kind of being* the *cross* is, the *kind of being* the *obedient* *man* accepting the *death* *penalty* is, *not* the *kind of being* the *separate* *event* is. The textual evidence is the *Isaiah 53:4–6*, *10–12* (the *Suffering Servant*), the *Romans 3:21–26* (the *propitiation*), the *2 Corinthians 5:21* (the *expiation*), the *Galatians 3:13* (the *redemption*), the *1 Peter 2:24* and *3:18* (the *Petrine* *cross*), and the *Hebrews 9* (the *blood* and *once for all*). The chapter rates the *cross* as `[E]` for the textual evidence; the *interpretation* as the *kind of being* the *payment* is `[I-high]`.

The chapter's *secondary* claim is the *active* *obedience* (Chapter 12) and the *death* (Chapter 14) are joined into *one* *inseparable* *claim* — the *kind of being* the *death* is, the *kind of being* the *culmination* of the *obedience* is. The *active* *obedience* is the *kind of being* the *Christ* is, the *kind of being* the *lifelong* *obedient* is (Chapter 12). The *death* is the *kind of being* the *Christ* is, the *kind of being* the *culmination* of the *obedience* is (Chapter 14). The two are *not* *separate* — the *death* is the *kind of being* the *obedience* is, *in the face of* the *death* *penalty*.

The chapter's *negative* claim is the *cross* is *not* the *separate* *event* from the *obedience* — the *cross* is the *kind of being* the *obedient* *man* accepting the *death* *penalty* is, *not* the *kind of being* the *separate* *event* is.
