Dear Dr. Smith,

Your *Wisdom Christology in the Gospel of John* and *A Systematic Theology of the Early Church* work the same ground my project stands on: a high human Christology argued from the biblical text rather than the later creeds. I am writing to ask whether you would consider giving the manuscript's load-bearing argument your eye.

Some honest context about who is asking. I am not an academic. I went to a Christian school as a kid, was raised Baptist and then non-denominational, and was always taught that the Bible is the final source of truth. Over the past five years or so, my questions about the logic of Father and Son became more frequent — and my church's answers stopped being able to carry them. This manuscript is an agentic work of my own developing thoughts and beliefs: drafted with AI assistance under my direction and a structured methodology, but the questions, the conclusions, and the theology are mine, and they are still developing. I am sending it to you because your published work is the closest scholarship to where my thinking has been heading, and I would rather have my developing thoughts corrected by someone who has done this rigorously than left to their own momentum.

I have drafted a complete manuscript, *The Debt-Debtor Thesis of Redemption* — a biblical theology of redemption from a Biblical Unitarian christology, seventeen chapters and three appendices, roughly 78,000 words. The working thesis: humanity incurred a debt to God through Adam's disobedience; God established death as the penalty; redemption required a corresponding human representative — the last Adam — whose lifelong willing obedience culminated in sacrificial death; and the Father vindicated him by resurrection.

Three features may be of interest to you specifically:

1. **A strict, auditable Bible-only method.** Every claim is tagged `[E]` (explicit text), `[I-band]` (strong implication), or `[H-band]` (hypothesis), and every non-explicit claim carries an evidence sketch with evidence-for, evidence-against, and a band justification. The boundary between what the text states and what the argument infers is checkable line by line.
2. **The load-bearing argument pair.** Chapter 11 argues why the redeemer must be human — the same-order correspondence drawn from the Adam–Christ parallel of Romans 5 and 1 Corinthians 15. Chapter 15 argues why one man's obedience suffices for many, from the one-act/many-recipients structure of Romans 5:18–19 and 1 Corinthians 15:20–22.
3. **Appendix B pressure-tests the hard high-Christology texts** — John 1:1, *monogenēs*, Philippians 2:6–11, Colossians 1:15–20, Hebrews 1, John 20:28, Titus 2:13, 2 Peter 1:1. Your Wisdom Christology argument — the Logos as personified Word/Wisdom rather than a preexistent divine person — is, to my reading, the strongest existing defense of the position my appendix sketches independently from the text alone. The appendix, per the project's Bible-only constraint, cites no secondary literature as evidence, so it does not cite your book — but it is the place where your argument and mine meet, and where your critique would be most decisive. The project also uses the Revised English Version among its reference translations.

I would be grateful for honest engagement with any single piece of it — the correspondence argument, the sufficiency mechanism, or the hard-texts appendix — whether as written comments, a conversation, or simply a pointer at the weakest link. The full manuscript is available in any form you prefer.

With respect and gratitude for your work,

Andrew Jochl
