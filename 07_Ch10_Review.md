# Review of Chapter 10 — Obedience Above Sacrifice

> **Reviewer pass:** 2026-08-07, against `chapters/Chapter_10_Obedience_Above_Sacrifice.md` (170 lines, ~5,300 words, eight numbered sections plus acceptance/verification).
> **Method:** read chapter end-to-end; cross-checked outline criteria (R10.1, R10.2, E10.1, E10.2, E10.3, W10.1, A10 in `01_Architecture_and_Outline.md`); verified all load-bearing quotations against esv.org (1 Sam 15:22–23; Hos 6:6; Matt 9:13; Matt 12:7; Isa 1:11, 13; Jer 7:21–23; Amos 5:21–24; Mic 6:6–7; Mic 6:8; Ps 51:16–17 — all confirmed word-for-word); cross-checked `[E]/[I]/[H]` tagging protocol against `INTRO.md` §I.5.
> **Scope:** This is a first-draft review. The chapter's argument and structure are sound; the chapter as drafted has substantial problems in (1) prose, (2) tagging protocol, (3) evidence-sketches, and (4) word-count discipline. It needs a second pass before acceptance. The diagnosis and proposed fix follow.

## Verdict

The chapter's *core* claim — that the OT prophetic critique rejects *sacrifice without obedience* and not *sacrifice itself* — is sound, textually grounded, and well within the project's framing. The eight passages cited (1 Sam 15; Hos 6:6; Matt 9:13 + 12:7; Isa 1; Jer 7; Amos 5; Mic 6; Ps 51) cover the canonical prophetic testimony cleanly, and the *covenant* framing the chapter uses (obedience is the covenant's primary demand; sacrifice is the institution that attends it) is a legitimate reading of the texts.

But the chapter as drafted has the same *first-draft* illness that Ch 7 had before its second pass: **heavy italic-emphasis template, repeated restatements, missing evidence sketches, and a word count that runs over the outline cap by ~4×**. The fix is the same as Ch 7's: strip the italics, write the claim once per section with proper weight, add evidence sketches for the `[I-*]` claims, cut the body to ~750–900 words. The argument survives; the prose does not.

A separate issue (call it 1.5) is that the chapter's *kind-of-being* template *inverts* the chapter's own argument in three places. The chapter says sacrifice *attends* the covenant but is not the covenant itself; the chapter writes that as "the *sacrifice* is the *kind of being* the *covenant* is *not*." That isn't a stylistic flaw — it reads as if sacrifice is being *defined* by its negation of the covenant, when the chapter's own point is that sacrifice is a *real institution* the prophets are not rejecting. The template is doing more harm than the chapter needs.

## 1. Prose — heavily overweighted

### 1.1 Italicization density: ~1 per 6–7 words

The same illness that Ch 7 had before its second pass. Every load-bearing word is wrapped in asterisks. The Ch 9 precedent and the Ch 11 rewrite (now plain-prose) set the house style: italics reserved for *defined terms* (e.g., *covenant*, *correspondence*, *last Adam*) and *chapter-specific key concepts*, not every other word. A representative sentence from §10.4 (line 51) of the current draft:

> "The *I desire mercy* language is the *kind of being* the *divine* *desire* is — the *kind of being* the *divine* *desire* is for the *kind of being* the *mercy* is. The *not sacrifice* language is the *kind of being* the *priority* is — the *kind of being* the *mercy* is *more than* the *kind of being* the *sacrifice* is."

Twelve italic spans in two sentences. By the time the reader reaches *is*, they have stopped registering emphasis.

**Fix:** Strip the asterisks. Reserve italics for (a) defined terms the chapter introduces (*covenant*, *sacrifice*, *obedience*, *prophetic critique* — the chapter's own four key concepts); (b) the *Hebrew* / *Greek* terms the translation notes need; (c) the book names the translation notes cite. Total italics in §10.4: ~2 spans, not 12. A 6× reduction brings the chapter in line with Ch 11's plain-prose style.

### 1.2 Repetition: same claim restated three or four times within a single section

Pattern recurs in §10.2 (1 Sam 15:22), §10.3 (Hos 6:6), §10.5 (Isa 1), §10.6 (Jer 7:21), §10.7 (Amos 5:21), §10.8 (Mic 6:6–8), §10.9 (Ps 51). The chapter reads as if each restatement is a new claim when it is the same claim with new words. Sample from §10.5 (lines 63–65):

> "The chapter reads the *divine* *weariness* as the *kind of being* the *sacrifice* *without obedience* is — the *kind of being* that *wears* the *divine* is the *kind of being* the *sacrifice* *without obedience* is."
> ...
> "The chapter treats the *iniquity* and *solemn assembly* as the *kind of being* the *covenant* *disobedience* is — the *kind of being* that *is* the *covenant* *disobedience* is the *kind of being* that *wears* the *divine*."

Same claim ("covenant disobedience is what the prophets reject, not sacrifice") twice in two paragraphs, with one new word (*wears*). Cut one. Keep the other. The chapter's information density is roughly a third of its word count.

**Fix:** Each passage section should make its claim *once* with proper weight, and then either move to the next passage or close with the synthesis. A section should be ~80–120 words, not 200–300. The restatements are the *writing* problem, not the *thinking* problem — the chapter knows what it wants to say; it just says each thing four times.

### 1.3 The "The X passage is the *most explicit* *Y* reading of the *Z* theme" template

Every per-passage section opens with the same formula: "*The 1 Samuel 15:22–23 passage is the foundational explicit textual witness to the obedience-above-sacrifice theme.*" (§10.2 line 21). "*The Hosea 6:6 passage is the most explicit prophetic formulation of the obedience-above-sacrifice theme*" (§10.3 line 33). "*The Jesus reading of the Hosea 6:6 passage is the most explicit post-canonical reaffirmation*" (§10.4 line 45). "*The Isaiah 1:11–17 passage is the most explicit prophetic critique of sacrifice without obedience*" (§10.5 line 59).

This template does work in section 1 — it tells the reader what to expect. Doing it in every section turns the chapter into a sevenfold recital. By the time the reader reaches §10.9, they know the chapter's argument is *obedience-above-sacrifice*. The reader does not need to be told.

**Fix:** Vary the openings. *1 Sam 15* can open with the *Saul* narrative (the king who kept the spoil and lost the kingdom); *Hos 6:6* can open with the *hesed* / *da'at* pairing; *Matt 9:13* can open with the *table-fellowship* context (the tax collectors); *Isa 1* can open with the *multitude of sacrifices*; *Jer 7* can open with the *add your burnt offerings to your sacrifices* irony; *Amos 5* can open with the *let justice roll* image; *Mic 6:6–8* can open with the *what shall I come before the Lord with* rhetorical question; *Ps 51* can open with the *against you, you only, have I sinned* David context. Each opening *earns* its place by showing what is unique in the passage; the chapter's structure shouldn't make them all sound the same.

### 1.4 The "The chapter treats X as the *kind of being* Y is" closing template

Every per-passage section closes with the same incantation: "*The chapter treats the X passage as the Y reading. The Y is the kind of being the Z is. The chapter rates the textual evidence as [E]; the interpretation as [I-high]*." This is documentation of the *chapter's* interpretive move, repeated eight times, in nearly identical language.

**Fix:** Move the rating decisions to a single section at the end of each per-passage treatment, or to the chapter's synthesis (§10.10). The body of each per-passage section should *make* the claim once; the synthesis should *state* what was claimed and *rate* it. The current draft does both in every section, doubling the word count without doubling the information.

### 1.5 The chapter's *kind-of-being* template *inverts* the argument in three places

This is the most important prose fix because it's not just stylistic. The chapter argues that sacrifice is a *real institution* the prophets are not rejecting. But the template writes that as "the *sacrifice* is the *kind of being* the *covenant* is *not*." That reads as if *sacrifice* is being defined by *what it is not* — a category mistake, not a covenantal argument. The three places:

- **§10.3 line 39** (Hos 6:6): "the *covenant* is *for* is the *kind of being* the *covenant* is *not* the *sacrifice* is." The chapter is trying to say "covenant is *for* steadfast love, not sacrifice" — but the template makes the covenant sound like it's defined by *not-sacrifice*. The fix: drop the negation; the verse itself says *I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice* — the *not* is in the Hebrew/Greek, not in the chapter's *kind-of-being* template.
- **§10.6 line 75** (Jer 7): "the *covenant* is *for* is *not* the *kind of being* the *sacrifice* is." Same inversion.
- **§10.5 line 65** (Isa 1): "the *iniquity* and *solemn assembly* as the *kind of being* the *covenant* is *for* — the *kind of being* the *covenant* is *for* is the *kind of being* the *iniquity* is *not*, and the *solemn assembly* is *not* the *covenant* itself." The chapter wants to say the prophets reject the *combination* of *iniquity + solemn assembly*. The template restates that as a triple-negative, which is hard to parse and reads as if the chapter is denying that the *covenant* is a real thing.

**Fix:** Replace the negated *kind-of-being* phrasing with positive covenantal phrasing. The chapter's actual claim is: "*the covenant is for steadfast love and the knowledge of God; sacrifice is the institution that attends the covenant but is not the covenant itself*." The "not" belongs in the chapter's voice, not in the template. A covenantal claim stated positively is clearer than one stated by negation of a template.

### 1.6 Forward-reference claims sit outside the verse sections

§10.10 (the chapter's "argument in summary") makes a forward-reference claim to Ch 11–14 (*the Last Adam*):

> "The chapter's *forward* reference is the *kind of being* the *covenant* is *for* is the *kind of being* the *Last Adam* (Chapters 11–14) is the *fulfillment* of."

That claim doesn't belong in the *summary* of Ch 10 — it belongs in the *transition* to Ch 11, in §10.10 only as a *pointer* ("the covenant is for obedience; the obedient Redeemer is the one the prophets pointed forward to"). The current draft makes it a *claim* about Ch 11, which the chapter cannot defend from the Ch 10 text. Cut it down to a pointer, or cut it entirely — the cross-link to Ch 12 is already in the acceptance check.

## 2. Logic — three overclaims and one framing problem

### 2.1 The *post-canonical* *reaffirmation* language for Matthew 9:13 and 12:7

§10.4 line 45 reads: "*The Jesus reading of the Hosea 6:6 passage is the most explicit post-canonical reaffirmation of the obedience-above-sacrifice theme.*" The phrase *post-canonical* is project jargon, but more importantly, *reaffirmation* is an interpretive claim — it says Jesus is *reaffirming* (i.e., re-applying) the Hosea text, not citing it neutrally. The text says he cites Hosea to ground his own table-fellowship argument; whether that's *reaffirmation* or *application* or *citation* is a matter the chapter doesn't adjudicate. Tagging it `[I-high]` and treating it as "Jesus reasserts the prophetic critique" is an overclaim.

**Fix:** Tag the *reaffirmation* claim as `[I-mid]` (it's a *reading* of why Jesus cites Hosea, not a transparent reading of the verse). The current draft rates it `[I-high]` (line 53), which is one notch too confident. Or: rename the section to "the Jesus citation" (descriptive) rather than "the Jesus *reaffirmation*" (interpretive). Let the verse do the work; let the chapter be lighter on the meta-language.

### 2.2 The Hosea 6:6 / Matthew 9:13 "kindred" claim

§10.4 line 55 reads: "*The Jesus reading replaces the Hosea ḥesed (steadfast love) with the Matthew eleos (mercy) — the kind of being the covenant is for is the kind of being the covenant love is. The two are kindred — the kind of being the covenant is for is the kind of being the covenant love is, in either Hebrew or Greek.*"

Three problems:

- **It treats translation choice as if it were a hermeneutical move.** Hosea uses *ḥesed*; Matthew uses *eleos*. That is a translation difference, not a "replacement." The Septuagint of Hosea 6:6 already uses *eleos* in many manuscripts, so the Matthew quote is just citing the LXX of Hosea, which is a *citation*, not a *replacement*. The current draft doesn't notice the LXX bridge.
- **"The two are kindred" is a claim the chapter doesn't defend.** The Hebrew *ḥesed* and Greek *eleos* have overlapping but non-identical semantic ranges (*ḥesed* is covenantal loyalty; *eleos* is more general compassion / mercy). Saying they are "kindred" is plausible but not self-evident; it requires either the LXX bridge or a separate semantic argument.
- **The tag is `[I-high]`, but the claim depends on contested translation and semantic-range data.** Per INTRO §I.5.2, `[I-high]` is for claims that survive most reasonable readings; the *ḥesed* / *eleos* overlap is *plausible* but requires the LXX argument to be `[I-high]`. Without it, this is `[I-mid]`.

**Fix:** Either (a) introduce the LXX bridge explicitly — "the LXX renders Hosea's *ḥesed* as *eleos*; Matthew cites the LXX, so the *kindred* claim is grounded in the LXX translation tradition" — which supports `[I-high]`; or (b) drop the *kindred* meta-claim entirely and treat §10.4 as "the Jesus citation shows the same *covenantal priority* the OT prophets named, now applied to table-fellowship" — which is `[I-mid]` and avoids the translation-overreach.

### 2.3 The *covenant* *love* is *kindred* — generic and not argumnentative

§10.4 line 55 includes the sentence: "*The two are kindred — the kind of being the covenant is for is the kind of being the covenant love is, in either Hebrew or Greek.*" This is the same kind of empty statement the Ch 7 review flagged. *Kindred* is not an argument. The chapter is making a *semantic* claim about Hebrew and Greek terms; the right way to make it is *semantically*, not via the *kind-of-being* template.

**Fix:** If the LXX bridge is introduced (per 2.2), the *kindred* claim is a *consequence* of the bridge, not an independent statement. Drop the standalone *kindred* sentence.

### 2.4 The chapter's *negative* claim (§10.10) is correct but under-argued

§10.10 line 117 says: "*The chapter's foregoing claim is the chapter does not argue the prophets reject sacrifice as an institution. The chapter argues the prophets reject sacrifice that is disconnected from the obedience the covenant is for.*" This is the chapter's most important negative claim — it's what keeps the chapter from being read as anti-sacrifice (which the outline's R10.2 explicitly warns against). But the chapter *only* states the claim in the summary; it doesn't *argue* it from the per-passage material.

The argument is actually present in the texts: the prophets critique the *combination* of sacrifice + disobedience (1 Sam 15: the *people spared the best to sacrifice*; Isa 1: *cannot endure iniquity and solemn assembly*; Amos 5: *even though you offer... I will not accept*; Mic 6: *thousands of rams* vs. *do justice*; Jer 7: *add your burnt offerings*). The chapter needs to point at this pattern in the synthesis, not just state the negative claim.

**Fix:** In §10.10, after the negative claim, add: "The pattern across the eight passages is consistent: sacrifice is named in the same breath as the disobedience, and the divine refusal is directed at the *combination*, not at the sacrifice. 1 Sam 15:21 — the people spared the spoil *to sacrifice*. Isa 1:13 — the new moon and Sabbath cannot be endured *with the iniquity*. Amos 5:22 — the offerings *will not be accepted*. Mic 6:6–7 — the rhetorical question of *what shall I bring* is met with the answer *do justice*. The combination is what the prophets reject; the institution is preserved." That paragraph is the chapter's R10.2 fulfillment; it should be in the body, not the summary.

## 3. Tagging protocol — every `[I-*]` is missing an evidence sketch

### 3.1 The chapter has 12 `[I-*]` claims and *zero* evidence sketches

`INTRO.md` §I.5.3 says explicitly: "*Every [I] and [H] claim in this paper is accompanied by a short evidence sketch of the following form: [Evidence for / Evidence against / Band justification].*" The chapter has *twelve* `[I-*]` tags (in §10.3, §10.4, §10.5, §10.6, §10.7, §10.8, §10.9, §10.10) and *zero* evidence sketches. This is the same direct protocol violation the Ch 7 review flagged. A reviewer cannot audit a claim without an evidence sketch.

**Fix:** Add evidence sketches for the seven `[I-high]` claims in §§10.3, 10.4, 10.6, 10.7, 10.8, 10.9, 10.10. Each sketch should be 3–6 lines: two or three "evidence for" points (specific Bible passages), one or two "evidence against / weakening" points, and a one-line band justification. Pattern: see the `[I-high]` sketch in Ch 11 §11.2 for the house style.

### 3.2 Most `[I-high]` claims are one notch too confident

The chapter's `[I-high]` claims (in §§10.3, 10.6, 10.7, 10.8, 10.9, 10.10) are mostly *the chapter's* readings of what the texts mean, not transparent statements of what the texts say. The most defensible are:

- §10.6 (Jer 7:21) — *I did not speak to your fathers concerning burnt offerings and sacrifices* is genuinely `I-high` for "the priority of obedience is the primary covenantal demand"
- §10.7 (Amos 5:21–24) — *I hate, I despise your feasts* is genuinely `I-high` for "the combination of feast + injustice is what the prophet rejects"
- §10.8 (Mic 6:8) — *do justice, love kindness, walk humbly* is genuinely `I-high` for "the threefold positive answer to the what-shall-I-bring question"

The least defensible (per 2.1–2.3 above) are:

- §10.4 (Matt 9:13) — *post-canonical reaffirmation* should be `I-mid`, not `I-high`
- §10.4 (Matt 9:13 / Hos 6:6 *kindred*) — *kindred* of *ḥesed* and *eleos* is `I-mid` without the LXX bridge
- §10.10 (synthesis-level claim) — the "the covenant is for justice, kindness, humility" synthesis should be `I-mid` because the chapter is generalizing from per-passage readings

**Fix:** Audit every `[I-high]` and downgrade to `[I-mid]` where the claim is the chapter's reading rather than a transparent statement of the text. The chapter's credibility depends on the tag system being honest.

### 3.3 The *foregoing* / *secondary* / *primary* language in §10.10 is the chapter's, not the tag system

§10.10 uses *primary claim*, *secondary claim*, and *foregoing claim* as scaffolding. The tag system uses `[E]` / `[I-band]` / `[H-band]`. These are *different* vocabularies. The chapter should use one consistently.

**Fix:** Either replace *primary* / *secondary* / *foregoing* with `[E]` / `[I-high]` / `[I-mid]` throughout the synthesis, or keep the *primary* / *secondary* / *foregoing* language and *also* tag every claim. The Ch 11 synthesis uses both (`[E]` for the verses, `[I-high]` for the inference). The current Ch 10 synthesis uses neither.

## 4. Reference handling — three problems

### 4.1 The verification log mis-describes uncited verses as cited in §10

The verification log (§10.12, lines 140–149) lists ten load-bearing quotations with citation §s, but two of the listed references are not actually cited in the chapter body:

- **Isaiah 1:18–20** is listed as "cited in §10.5 as the covenant response to the prophetic critique" — but the §10.5 prose does not include this passage. It is in the verification log but not in the body.
- **Mark 12:33** is listed as "cited in §10.8 as the Scribe reading of the Micah 6:8 passage" — but the §10.8 prose does not include this passage. It is in the verification log but not in the body.

These are not *errors* — the verses are *useful* for the chapter's argument. But the verification log is misleading about where they appear.

**Fix:** Either (a) add the verses to the body prose with a short treatment, or (b) correct the verification log to "verified; not cited in the chapter prose; available for the Ch 12 reading of Christ's active obedience" (the reference is a *forward link* to Ch 12, not a *citation* in Ch 10). Option (b) is the lighter edit and doesn't require the chapter to grow.

### 4.2 The translation note 1 on *ṭôb* / *ḥesed* is more confusing than clarifying

§10.12 line 172 (translation note 1) reads: "*the better (Hebrew ṭôb) is the preference — the kind of being the covenant is for is the kind of being the obedience is, not the kind of being the sacrifice is.*"

This translation note *introduces* the *kind-of-being* template into the technical-notes section, where the reader expects lexical information, not interpretive language. The note is doing the same thing the body is doing. The reader looking up *ṭôb* should find: "*ṭôb* = good/better; the comparative is the standard Hebrew way of stating preference" — not the chapter's *kind-of-being* claim.

**Fix:** Strip the *kind-of-being* language from all five translation notes. Each note should be a clean lexical entry. The interpretive claim belongs in the body, not in the notes.

### 4.3 The Hebrew of *ḥesed* is not the LXX of *eleos* in the note

Translation note 2 (line 174) treats *ḥesed* (Hebrew) and *eleos* (Greek) as the same lexical item, but *ḥesed* in the LXX of Hosea 6:6 is often rendered *eleos* — so Matthew's *eleos* in Matt 9:13 is *citing the LXX* of Hosea, not *translating* the Hebrew *ḥesed* afresh. The current note misses this LXX bridge, which is what the *kindred* claim of §10.4 actually rests on (per 2.2 above).

**Fix:** Add to translation note 2: "*ḥesed* (Hebrew, 'covenantal loyalty / steadfast love') is rendered *eleos* (Greek, 'mercy / compassion') in the LXX of Hosea 6:6. Matthew 9:13 cites the LXX form; the *kindred* claim between *ḥesed* and *eleos* is therefore a claim about the LXX translation tradition, not a translation choice of Matthew's own." That grounds the *kindred* claim in the textual bridge and supports the `[I-high]` rating (per 2.2 fix).

## 5. Word count and structure

### 5.1 W10.1 specifies 600–900 words; the chapter is ~3,100 words body

The outline (W10.1) caps the chapter body at 600–900 words. The chapter is ~3,100 words body, ~3.5x the cap. The bulk is (a) the italic-overweight prose, (b) the restatements within each per-passage section, (c) the *foregoing* / *secondary* / *primary* synthesis at §10.10, which itself runs ~250 words.

The cap matters because A10 is "the chapter answers *what kind of obedience does God require?* and supplies the language for Christ's active obedience in Chapter 12" — a focused 700-word chapter can make this point clearly; a 3,100-word chapter buries it. The same logic that produced the Ch 7 second pass applies here.

**Fix:** Cut the chapter body to ~750 words. The per-passage sections (8 passages) should run ~60–80 words each (480–640 words total); §10.10 synthesis ~80 words; §10.1 the-question ~50 words. Total ~600–770 words. The §10.12 verification log and translation notes can stay — they are not subject to W10.1.

### 5.2 The 17 reference-only passages in §10.12 are excessive

The verification log (§10.12, lines 151–168) lists 17 reference-only passages. That's a *lot* of background for a 750-word chapter. The Ch 7 verification log lists 6 reference-only passages; the Ch 11 verification log lists 6. The Ch 10 list at 17 is roughly 3x the project standard.

**Fix:** Keep the 5 reference-only passages that are *most directly* load-bearing: Lev 17:11 (the blood-atonement baseline that the prophets are not rejecting); Isa 53 (the Suffering Servant forward reference to Ch 14); Heb 10:5–9 (the Christological *doing the will* language); Ps 40:6–8 (the David-Messianic *doing your will* parallel); Prov 21:3 (the *doing righteousness and justice is more acceptable than sacrifice* parallel). Drop the other 12 unless they earn their place in the prose. A focused reference list is more auditable than a long one.

### 5.3 Section balance is off: §10.10 is longer than it needs to be

§10.10 (synthesis) is currently ~250 words and makes the *primary*, *secondary*, *foregoing*, and *forward* claims, each in its own paragraph. The *foregoing* claim (chapter does not argue anti-sacrifice) is the most important; the *forward* claim (Last Adam fulfillment) is a pointer, not a claim. The synthesis can be one paragraph ~80 words that names the *primary* claim, the *foregoing* claim (the negative), and a *pointer* to Ch 12.

**Fix:** Cut §10.10 to one tight paragraph. Move the *foregoing* / *primary* / *forward* language to a single sentence each. The synthesis shouldn't be longer than any per-passage section.

## 6. Cross-references — Ch 9 link is in the acceptance check, not the body

### 6.1 The Ch 9 cross-link is documented in the acceptance check but not in the body

The acceptance check (§10.11, line 132) says: "*Cross-link to Chapter 9 — the prophetic critique is the kind of being the covenant recipient is: made in §10.1*." But the §10.1 prose (line 17) does not *name* the Ch 9 *pattern* / *constitutional transmission* / *ontic* / *forensic* axes. It just says the chapter confines itself to the prophetic critique. A reader who hasn't read Ch 9 won't see the connection.

**Fix:** Add a one-sentence cross-link in §10.1: "This is the chapter that completes the *pattern of disobedience* Chapter 9 established at the covenant scale; the *prophetic critique* is the divine response to the pattern — the pattern the prophets name is *sacrifice without obedience* (Isa 1:13; Amos 5:21; Mic 6:6–8)." That one sentence makes the Ch 9 link visible in the body, not just the acceptance check.

### 6.2 The Ch 12 forward-link is in the body but not the acceptance check's "Forward cross-link" entry

§10.10 line 119 makes the *forward* claim to the *Last Adam* (Chapters 11–14). The acceptance check (§10.11) has a *Forward cross-link to Chapter 12* entry (line 131), but that entry refers to §10.8 (Micah 6:8) as the language for Christ's active obedience. The body actually makes the forward-link at §10.10 (the *Last Adam* claim), not at §10.8. The acceptance check is mis-attributed.

**Fix:** Update the acceptance check's *Forward cross-link* entry to point at §10.10 (where the *Last Adam* claim is made), not §10.8 (where Mic 6:8 is read).
